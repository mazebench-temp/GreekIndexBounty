# Odyssey 6: research limitations and leads

Author: RawNuke

Date: 2026-10-07. The sources audit wrote this file. It lists the sources that the research did not open, the page numbers that come only from OCR, and the other known limits. The consulted sources are in `sources.json`. The items below are not consulted sources, and the ledger does not log them as consulted.

## 1. Leads that were not opened

These works are relevant to Book 6. The research did not read them. The articles do not cite page numbers from them.

### Modern commentaries

1. A. Heubeck, S. West and J. B. Hainsworth, *A Commentary on Homer's Odyssey*, vol. 1 (Oxford 1988). J. B. Hainsworth wrote the notes on Books 5-8. Use: notes on 6.4, 6.8, 6.48, 6.201, 6.242, 6.244-245, 6.265.
2. A. F. Garvie, *Homer: Odyssey Books VI-VIII* (Cambridge 1994). Use: the same lines and the Nausicaa episode.
3. I. J. F. de Jong, *A Narratological Commentary on the Odyssey* (Cambridge 2001). Only the review by R. Scodel (BMCR 2002.06.12) was read.
4. G. Danek, *Epos und Zitat* (Vienna 1998). The Scodel review names it.

### Editions and critical tools

5. M. L. West (ed.), *Homerus: Odyssea* (Teubner 2017). Use: a modern apparatus for 6.201, 6.242, 6.244-245, 6.275-288.
6. H. van Thiel (ed.), *Homeri Odyssea* (1991). Same use.
7. A. Ludwich (ed.), *Homeri Odyssea*, editio maior (1889-1891). The editio minor was opened. It has no apparatus for Book 6.
8. F. Pontani (ed.), *Scholia Graeca in Odysseam* (Rome 2007-). This edition replaces Dindorf for the books that it covers. The research used Dindorf (1855) only. The manuscript sigla in the articles are Dindorf's sigla.
9. K. Lehrs, *De Aristarchi studiis Homericis* (3rd ed. 1882). The archive.org OCR is too poor to find the pages that Dindorf and Merry and Riddell cite. The articles report Lehrs only as Merry and Riddell or Dindorf report him.

### Fragment collections

10. Alcman: the line quoted in the scholion on 6.244 ("Ζεῦ πάτερ, αἲ γὰρ ἐμὸς πόσις εἴη"). The fragment number in Page, *PMG*, or Davies, *PMGF*, was not checked. The article on Alcman says this.
11. Ephorus (scholion on 6.244). The fragment number in Jacoby, *FGrHist* 70, was not checked. The text of Ephorus was not consulted.
12. Simonides, *Kateuchai* (scholion on 6.164). The fragment number was not checked.
13. Sophocles, *Nausicaa* or *Plyntriai*, frr. 439-441 Radt (*TrGF* 4). Radt's edition was not opened. The articles take the fragment numbers, the double title and the date from Carrara 2022, and they say so.
14. Agallis of Corcyra, Dicaearchus and Hippasus (Athenaeus 1.14d). The FHG numbers come from the Kaibel text in the Perseus file only.
15. Callimachus on Corcyra and Scheria (Strabo 7.3.6; Pliny 4.52). The fragment number in Pfeiffer was not checked.
16. G. Wustmann, *Rheinisches Museum* 23 (1868) 225-247, at p. 238. The research knows this article only from Chernysheva 2017.
17. Philyllius, *Plyntriai or Nausicaa*, and Eubulus, *Nausicaa* (K.-A.). Known only from Carrara 2022.

### Modern books known only from reviews or abstracts

19. S. E. Psoma, *Corcyra* (2022). Only BMCR 2024.05.19 was read.

### Lexica and ancient texts that were not opened

24. R. J. Cunliffe, *A Lexicon of the Homeric Dialect*; P. Chantraine, *DELG*; R. Beekes, *EDG*; *Lexikon des frühgriechischen Epos*. Use: ἀλφηστής, διερός, δέατο, θρόνα, ἐπίστιον.

27. Julian, 291 B (Spanheim). Known from the apparatus of Monro and Allen. The article says that Julian's text was not checked.
28. Aristotle, fragments (Rose). Only the *Rhetoric*, the *Poetics* and *History of Animals* 6.31 and 8.28 were searched.
29. Callimachus, *Hymn to Delos*, on the palm of Leto.
30. The court records of the 1920 prosecution of *The Little Review*. The article on Joyce's "Nausicaa" reports the prosecution from Wikipedia and says that the records were not consulted.
31. The Durham "Living Poets" page for Tennyson's "Lucretius" (publication data). A search result showed the page title. The page did not open.

## 2. Page numbers that come from OCR only

The page images were read for these pages: Dindorf pp. 293, 294, 295, 296, 297, 298, 302, 303, 304, 307, 308, 311, 314, 316, 317, 318; Merry and Riddell pp. 263, 274; Eustathius p. 241; Monro and Allen leaves n126 and n127 (apparatus). The research pass reports that it read Dindorf pp. 1-2 (leaves n76-n77) and Monro and Allen leaf n123 on the images. The sources audit did not have those images.

All other page numbers come from the archive.org OCR text. The audit matched them to the archive.org page index, or to a running head in the OCR, or to the page break before the next page image. These pages are:

- Dindorf, *Scholia Graeca in Homeri Odysseam*, vol. 1: pp. 24-25, 62, 299, 300, 301, 305, 306, 309, 310, 312, 313, 315, 319, 320, 325.
- Merry and Riddell, *Homer's Odyssey*, vol. 1 (2nd ed. 1886): pp. 39-41, 212-213, 253-262, 264-273, 275-281, 286, 319.
- Eustathius, ed. Stallbaum, vol. 1: pp. 234, 236, 237, 240, 242, 248, 251, 252, 253, and the preface.
- Monro and Allen, *Homeri Opera* vol. 3 (1908): leaves n124, n125, n128-n130. This edition prints no page numbers on these leaves. The ledger cites archive.org leaf numbers.

The OCR of Greek has errors. One example: the OCR of Dindorf p. 314 reads "Δεῦ πάτερ" in the Alcman quotation. The page image reads "Ζεῦ πάτερ". The articles follow the page image.

## 3. Other limits

1. The scholia are cited from Dindorf (1855). Dindorf numbers the note on the palm as 6.163, and the articles use that number. The modern edition of Pontani was not used.
2. The Perseus XML of Aristotle's *Rhetoric* labels the quotation of Od. 6.327 as "Od. 7.327". The articles give 6.327.
3. The Casaubon page letters for Athenaeus 1.14d and 1.14f come from LSJ citations. The Perseus file gives the Kaibel chapters only. Carrara 2022 cites the Sophocles sentence as Epitome 1.20F (Kaibel 1.45.21-2). The exact Casaubon letter of that sentence was not confirmed from a printed edition.
4. Strabo, Pausanias, Thucydides, Apollonius, Plutarch, Aristotle and the Latin authors are cited from the Perseus digital editions. The audit checked the section numbers in the TEI files. It did not compare them with printed editions.
5. The life dates in the new author entries come from English Wikipedia (accessed 2026-10-07) and, for Alcman, from Smith's *Dictionary of Greek and Roman Biography* (1849). The dates of Alcman and Polygnotus are approximate in all sources.
6. The date 427 BC for Thucydides 3.70 comes from the narrative order of Thucydides' third book (the fifth year of the war). Thucydides does not state the year in the sentence.
7. The Project Gutenberg text of *Ulysses* (eBook 4300) does not state which printing it follows. It marks the episodes with bracketed numbers. The 1922 edition has no episode titles.
8. The sources audit merged near-duplicate records of the same work and locator. A merged record keeps the findings of each original record. Some merged findings therefore repeat a point in different words.
9. Wikipedia is a tertiary source. The ledger uses it only for life dates, for the publication facts of *Ulysses*, and for the prosecution of 1920.
