summary: Book 6 has one formal reply verse, the Odyssey's other answering frame, τὸν δʼ αὖ … ἀντίον ηὔδα, “then … answered him face to face” (6.186). Nausicaa is the only woman who answers with this frame in the Odyssey.
grc: ["ἀντίον ηὔδα"]
en: [answered him face to face]
---

**The verse.** τὸν δʼ αὖ Ναυσικάα λευκώλενος ἀντίον ηὔδα, “then white-armed Nausicaa answered him face to face” (6.186). The frame is τὸν (τὴν) δʼ αὖ (“and to him in turn”) + the speaker's name and epithet + ἀντίον ηὔδα, “spoke in answer, face to face”: ἀντίον is the neuter of ἀντίος, “opposite”, used as an adverb, and ηὔδα is the imperfect of αὐδάω, “speak”. It does the work of [[then-answered]], with the speaker's name in the middle of the verse.

**Who uses it.** The frame is far more common in the Odyssey than in the Iliad. In the Odyssey it introduces 55 replies, and 43 of them are Telemachus' (τὸν δʼ αὖ Τηλέμαχος πεπνυμένος ἀντίον ηὔδα, first at 1.213). The other speakers are men: the suitors Eurymachus and Leocritus, Nestor's son Peisistratus, Noemon, Peiraeus, and Laertes; and once Zeus, speaking to Hermes (5.28). Nausicaa is the one woman. Her name fills the place that Telemachus' name fills, and her epithet λευκώλενος, “white-armed”, takes the place of his πεπνυμένος, “wise” (see [[white-armed]] and [[nausicaa]]).

**Her answer.** She is replying to Odysseus' long supplication (6.149–185; see [[supplication-type-scene]]). Her first sentence begins with a reason, “Stranger, since you seem like neither a base man nor a senseless one”, and leaves it without a main clause (6.187; see [[anacoluthon]]), turns to a maxim about Zeus, who deals out prosperity to good and bad as he wishes (6.188–190; see [[zeus-deals-out-prosperity]]), and ends with the promise of clothes, guidance to the city and the name of her people (6.191–197).
