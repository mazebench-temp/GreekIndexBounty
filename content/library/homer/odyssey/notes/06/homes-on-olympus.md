---
summary: Nausicaa says that the stranger has come by the will of “the gods who hold Olympus” (οἳ Ὄλυμπον ἔχουσιν, 6.240), the verse-end sister of the entry's formula.
---

“Not against the will of all the gods who hold Olympus / does this man mingle with the godlike Phaeacians” (6.240–241; [[antitheos]]). The verse-end formula θεῶν, οἳ Ὄλυμπον ἔχουσιν occurs also at 8.331, 12.337 and 19.43, and with τοί for οἵ at 14.394 and 18.180. See [[olympus]] and [[nausicaa]].

Book 6 has described the home of these gods. When Athena leaves the dream she goes to Olympus, where the seat of the gods is never shaken by winds, wet with rain or touched by snow (6.41–46). The same speech of Nausicaa uses a second formula for the gods: the stranger is now “like the gods who hold the wide sky” (6.243), the formula that Odysseus used in his supplication (6.150).
