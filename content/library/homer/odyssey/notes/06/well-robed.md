---
summary: Dawn wakes “well-robed Nausicaa” (6.49) after a dream about clothes.
grc: [ἐΰπεπλον]
---

“At once came Dawn of the fair throne, who woke her, / well-robed Nausicaa; and immediately she wondered at the dream” (6.48–49; [[of-the-fair-throne]], [[dream]]). In the dream Athena told her that her fine clothes lay uncared for and that her wedding was near, and sent her to wash belts, robes (πέπλους) and bright coverlets (6.25–38). The epithet names her by the robe on the morning that is given to the washing of robes. See [[nausicaa]].

The only other use of ἐΰπεπλος in the Odyssey is of the Achaean women (21.160).
