---
summary: By the Posideion the Phaeacians “tend the tackle of the black ships” (6.268).
grc: ["νηῶν ὅπλα μελαινάων"]
---

“There they tend the tackle of the black ships, / cables and sails, and they taper the oars” (6.268–269). The place is the assembly ground around the Posideion, paved with stones set into the earth (6.266–267; [[posideion]]). The verses show the Phaeacian city at its everyday work, the care of ships. The following verses say that bows and quivers are nothing to them (6.270–272; [[balanced-ships]]). See [[ships]] and [[phaeacians]].

The genitive plural μελαινάων stands apart from νηῶν, with ὅπλα between them. The pattern for this note is the whole phrase, because μελαινάων alone also describes pains (μελαινάων ὀδυνάων, Il. 4.191; [[black-pains]]).
