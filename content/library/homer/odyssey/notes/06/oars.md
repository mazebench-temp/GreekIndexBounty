---
summary: The Phaeacians shape their oars at the place of assembly and glory in the oars of their ships.
grc: [ἐρετμά]
---

Nausicaa names the oars twice in her picture of the harbor. At the place of assembly the men tend the tackle of the ships, “and they taper the oars” (ἀποξύνουσιν ἐρετμά, 6.269). The Phaeacians do not care for the bow or the quiver, “but for masts and the oars of ships and balanced ships” (6.270–271). Merry and Riddell compare ἀποξύνουσιν with the epithet προήκεα, “tapering”, given to oars at Odyssey 12.205 (note on 6.269). See [[ships]], [[mast]] and [[phaeacians]].
