---
summary: Sheep appear in Book 6 only in the lion simile. The hungry lion goes among the cattle or the sheep, and his belly bids him try for the flocks even in the close-built fold.
grc: [ὀίεσσιν]
---

The lion to which the naked Odysseus is compared “goes among the cattle or the sheep or after the wild deer; and his belly bids him make an attempt on the sheep, and go even into the close-built fold” (6.132–134; see [[the-lion-and-the-girls]] and [[cattle]]). Line 6.134 repeats Iliad 12.301 with γαστήρ, “belly”, in place of θυμὸς ἀγήνωρ, “his proud spirit” (see the translation notes, [[gaster]] and [[the-mountain-lion-and-the-fold]]). The sheep are named twice, ὀίεσσιν (6.132) and μήλων (6.134), the second word covering the small livestock of the fold. The guarded fold of the simile answers the girls on the shore, whom Odysseus must approach out of need (6.135–136).
