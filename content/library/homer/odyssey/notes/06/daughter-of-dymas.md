Athena comes to Nausicaa's bed “like a breath of wind”, stands above her head, and speaks “in the likeness of the daughter of Dymas” (6.20–24; see [[like-a-breath-of-wind]] and [[dream-scene]]). The pattern is old: the Dream sent by Zeus stands above Agamemnon's head “in the likeness of the son of Neleus” (Iliad 2.20), and the phantom that Athena sends to Penelope takes the shape of her sister Iphthime and stands above her head (4.795–803). In each case the disguise is a person whom the sleeper trusts.

The choice of a girl of the same age sets the tone of the speech:

{{quote:Od. 6.25-28 | Why did your mother bear you so slack?}}

The disguised goddess scolds as a friend may scold. She calls Nausicaa μεθήμων, “slack”, speaks of her marriage, of the good report that pleases her parents, and of the suitors, and proposes a shared outing: “let us go to wash them”, and “I will go along with you as a fellow worker” (συνέριθος, 6.31–32; see [[nausicaa]] and [[phemis]]). The first-person plural is the friend's voice. The promise of company is not kept in the story: the friend does not come to the river, and Athena acts there unseen (6.112–117).

Nausicaa wakes and “immediately she wondered at the dream” (6.49). She goes through the house “to tell it to her parents” (6.50), but in her request to her father she speaks only of the washing (6.57–65; see [[onar]] and [[athena]]).
