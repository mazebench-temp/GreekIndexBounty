---
summary: Nausicaa promises the naked stranger the things that are due to “a much-tried suppliant” (6.193). In Book 14 Eumaeus says the same two verses to Odysseus in his beggar's disguise.
---

Nausicaa answers Odysseus' supplication. Since he has come to their land, “you will not lack clothing, then, or anything else / of what befits a much-tried suppliant who has met with people” (6.192–193). With these words she accepts him as a [[supplication|suppliant]] and promises him clothes, and she adds that she will show him the city (6.194). See [[nausicaa]] and [[xenia]].

{{quote:3a3cd818-803d-5ea7-b904-d5ac12f30680}}

The two verses are a formula of the host. In Book 14 Eumaeus speaks them almost word for word to Odysseus, who is disguised as a beggar, after his story of the cloak at Troy: “so you will not lack clothing or anything else / of what befits a much-tried suppliant who has met with people” (14.510–511). Both times Odysseus is in need of clothes. Both times the host is someone below the rank of the hero: a girl here, and a slave in Book 14.

The epithet also fits the man. Odysseus has told her that he came from Ogygia on the twentieth day, carried by the waves and storms (6.170–172; [[ogygia]]). In Book 7 he uses the word of himself, when he asks Athena in disguise the way to the palace: “for I too have come here as a much-tried stranger, from a far-off land” (7.24–25). See [[much-enduring]] and [[odysseus]].
