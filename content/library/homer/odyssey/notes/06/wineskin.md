---
summary: Arete pours wine into a goatskin for her daughter's day at the river, with a chest of food.
---

Nausicaa's mother packs the provisions: “satisfying food of every kind” in a chest, relishes, and wine poured “in a goatskin” (ἐν δʼ οἶνον ἔχευεν / ἀσκῷ ἐν αἰγείῳ, 6.76–78; see [[queen-arete]], [[wine]] and [[food-and-drink]]). The half-verse ἀσκῷ ἐν αἰγείῳ is the one used of the wine that the Trojan heralds carry for the oath (Iliad 3.247). Here it carries the wine for a meal of girls by the river (6.97–99). The same provisions feed Odysseus when Nausicaa tells her maids to give the stranger “food and drink” (6.209, 6.246–248).
