---
summary: Robes, πέπλοι, are part of the laundry of the house that Nausicaa takes to the river.
grc: [πέπλους]
en: [robes]
---

The laundry that Athena tells Nausicaa to take to the river is “the sashes and the robes and the glossy coverlets” (ζῶστρά τε καὶ πέπλους καὶ ῥήγεα σιγαλόεντα, 6.38; see [[clothes]] and [[coverlets]]). πέπλος is the other Homeric word for a woman's robe beside ἑανός. LSJ defines it as any woven cloth used as a covering, and especially the upper garment worn by women, made in one piece. Nausicaa herself is ἐύπεπλος, “well-robed”, when Dawn wakes her (6.49; see [[well-robed]]). The most famous πέπλος in Homer is the robe the Trojan women lay on the knees of Athena's statue (Iliad 6.288–311; see [[the-robe-and-the-panathenaea]]).
