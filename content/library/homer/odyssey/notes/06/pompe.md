The word occurs once in the book, at the end of Nausicaa's long speech on the road to the city:

{{quote:92d1bb63-b892-5a3d-a99c-0b2a769bc133}}

This is the first promise of the escort that Zeus foretold in Book 5: the Phaeacians “will send him in a ship” to his own land (5.37). LSJ cites this line for the sense “sending home”. Nausicaa does not promise the escort herself. It is “from my father” (παρὰ πατρὸς ἐμοῖο), because the king gives it and the people man the ship (see [[alcinous]]). The pair πομπῆς καὶ νόστοιο names the means and the end (see [[nostos]]).

The book has already shown the means. As Nausicaa describes the way into the city, she names the harbor on each side, the curved ships drawn up along the road, each man with his own berth, and the men who tend tackle and taper oars (6.262–269; see [[epistion]]). The Phaeacians “do not care for the bow or the quiver, / but for masts and the oars of ships and balanced ships” (6.270–271). In the next two books Odysseus asks for the escort (7.151), Alcinous promises it (7.191–196, 7.317), and the Phaeacians give it in Book 13.
