---
summary: Wine goes into the goatskin for the girls' meal by the river, and the king drinks his wine on his throne by the hearth.
grc: [οἰνοποτάζει]
---

Nausicaa's mother pours wine into a goatskin for the day at the river (ἐν δʼ οἶνον ἔχευεν / ἀσκῷ ἐν αἰγείῳ, 6.77–78; see [[wineskin]] and [[food-and-drink]]). The handmaids later set “food and drink” before Odysseus from these provisions (6.246–248).

In Nausicaa's directions the king sits on his throne by the hearth, “on which he sits and drinks his wine like an immortal” (τῷ ὅ γε οἰνοποτάζει ἐφήμενος ἀθάνατος ὥς, 6.309; see [[throne]] and [[alcinous]]). οἰνοποτάζω, “drink wine”, occurs in Homer three times. Apollo, in the shape of Lycaon, reminds Aeneas of the boasts he made to the Trojan kings “while drinking wine” (Iliad 20.83–85), and Telemachus seats the disguised Odysseus among the men “drinking wine” (Odyssey 20.262; LSJ s.v.). The picture of the king at his wine explains why the stranger is to pass him by and go to the queen (6.310). The phrase οἴνοπα πόντον, “wine-dark sea” (6.170), is not a mention of wine.
