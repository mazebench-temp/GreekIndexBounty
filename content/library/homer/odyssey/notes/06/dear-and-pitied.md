summary: The last request of Book 6. In Athena's grove Odysseus asks the goddess to let him come to the Phaeacians “dear and pitied” (6.327), with Priam's words before he went to Achilles. The two words gather what he has asked of Nausicaa and what she has told him to win from her mother.

{{quote:65f8fe2a-370e-51af-a10a-13d1426bfcf5}}

**The prayer.** The verse is the third part of Odysseus' prayer: invocation (6.324), reproach for the goddess' absence in the storm (6.325–326), request (6.327). Athena hears him (6.328), but does not yet show herself, out of respect for Poseidon (6.329–331; see [[prayer-type-scene]], [[hear-me]] and [[athena]]).

**Pity.** “Pitied” takes up the plea he made to Nausicaa on the shore: “But, queen, have pity (ἐλέαιρε); for it is to you, after I have toiled through many evils, that I have come first” (6.175–176; see [[pity]] and [[supplication-type-scene]]). Nausicaa answered that he would lack nothing that befits a much-tried suppliant (6.191–193).

**Dear.** “Dear” takes up her instructions. She told him to pass by her father and clasp her mother's knees, and added: “If she is minded in friendship (φίλα φρονέῃσʼ) toward you in her heart, / then there is hope for you to see your dear ones” (φίλους, 6.310–315; see [[queen-arete]]). His request asks the goddess for the goodwill on which, Nausicaa says, everything depends.

**Priam's verse.** The verse is Iliad 24.309, with “the Phaeacians” for “Achilles” (see the entry above). In both places a man about to enter a stranger's house as a suppliant prays for the same two things. Priam went to the man who had killed his son. Odysseus goes to a people whose meeting place stands around a temple of Poseidon (6.266), the god who, as the narrator says at once, rages against him (6.330–331).

**The answer in the next books.** In Book 7 Odysseus clasps the knees of Arete and asks for passage home (7.142–152). At the start of Book 8 Athena makes him taller and sturdier and pours grace on his head and shoulders, “so that he might become dear (φίλος) to all the Phaeacians” (8.18–23; see [[gold-poured-on-silver]]).
