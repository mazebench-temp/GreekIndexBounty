---
summary: Deer appear twice in Book 6, both times in similes. Artemis delights in boars and swift deer on the mountains, and the hungry lion goes after the wild deer.
grc: [ἐλάφοισι, ἐλάφους]
---

In the simile that compares Nausicaa among her handmaids to Artemis among her nymphs, the goddess ranges the mountains of Taygetus and Erymanthus “delighting in wild boars and swift deer” (κάπροισι καὶ ὠκείῃς ἐλάφοισι, 6.104; see [[artemis-among-her-nymphs]], [[artemis]] and [[boar]]). The deer are her quarry, as in the Iliad, where Artemis teaches Scamandrius to shoot every wild thing of the mountains (Iliad 5.51–52).

In the lion simile that introduces Odysseus, the mountain-bred lion “goes among the cattle or the sheep or after the wild deer” (ἠὲ μετʼ ἀγροτέρας ἐλάφους, 6.132–133; see [[the-lion-and-the-girls]] and [[lion]]). The lines repeat the Iliad's simile of Sarpedon at the wall (Iliad 12.299–301; see [[the-mountain-lion-and-the-fold]]), and the deer belong to the list of prey there as here. In Book 6 the prey of the simile stands for the girls on the shore, who scatter in fear (6.138).
