---
summary: The Book 6 scholia preserve about ten judgments of Aristarchus. He athetized Nausicaa's wish for a husband (6.244–245), denied that Scheria was Corcyra, explained διερός as “living”, and chose several readings and accents that Murray's text follows, among them ῥεῖά τʼ at 6.108 and σὺ δʼ ὦκʼ at 6.289.
---

From the scholia (ed. Dindorf, vol. 1; page numbers in brackets):

- **6.8.** The Aristarchean copies read εἷσεν δὲ Σχερίῃ, “and he settled them in Scheria”, not εἷσεν δʼ ἐν Σχερίῃ. A note in the form used for his observations adds that the land of the Phaeacians was called Scheria and not Corcyra, and lies outside the inhabited world (p. 294). Murray follows the reading. See [[corcyra-and-the-phaeacians]].
- **6.89.** He accented the preposition πάρα with anastrophe, ποταμὸν πάρα, “beside the river”, because he joined prepositions to the more important words (ms. P, p. 301). Murray prints πάρα.
- **6.108.** The Aristarchean copies “and nearly all” read ῥεῖά τʼ, with τε (mss. H.P., p. 303). Monro and Allen record τʼ as the reading of Aristarchus against δʼ in the manuscripts. Murray prints τʼ.
- **6.131.** The copies of Aristarchus wrote εἶσι, “goes”, in full in the lion simile (mss. H.P., p. 305). See [[the-lion-and-the-girls]].
- **6.201.** He took διερὸς βροτός as “a living mortal”. Callistratus wrote δυερός, “wretched” (p. 311). See [[dieros]].
- **6.244–245.** He athetized both lines of Nausicaa's wish for a husband like the stranger, but hesitated over the first, because Alcman had adapted it (mss. H.Q., p. 314). See [[nausicaas-wish-244-245]].
- **6.287.** A note in ms. Q, under his name, asks for a smooth breathing on ἥ τʼ. Buttmann, quoted by Dindorf, suspected that Aristarchus wrote ἠδʼ, “and”. Dindorf thought that the name had been carried over from the note on 6.289 (p. 317, notes).
- **6.289.** σὺ δʼ ὦκʼ ἐμέθεν, “and you, quickly, hear my word” (ms. H, p. 317). Monro and Allen record ὦκʼ as Aristarchus' reading against ὧδʼ, “thus”, in the manuscripts. Murray prints ὦκʼ. Merry and Riddell suggest that he took it from Iliad 2.26, νῦν δʼ ἐμέθεν ξύνες ὦκα (p. 278).
- **6.330.** He accented ἐπιζαφελῶς, “vehemently”, with a circumflex, “and so it prevailed”. The scholiast says that it should have had a grave accent (p. 320). Murray prints ἐπιζαφελῶς.

The scholion on 6.256 notes that all copies have ἐμοῦ, not ἐμεῦ, and that Zenodotus sometimes changes such forms for the worse (p. 314). The note uses ὅτι, “(the sign is placed) because”, the form in which Aristarchus' signs were explained. See [[zenodotus]].

The anonymous athetesis of 6.275–288 is not ascribed to him in the scholion (see [[the-gossip-lines-275-288]]). No scholion ascribes to him the variant δόατʼ at 6.242 (see [[deato-or-doato]]).
