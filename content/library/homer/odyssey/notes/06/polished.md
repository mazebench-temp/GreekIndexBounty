---
summary: Nausicaa lays the clothes “on the polished carriage” (ἐυξέστῳ ἐπʼ ἀπήνῃ, 6.75). ἐΰξεστος is a synonym of the entry's ἐΰξοος.
---

“And the girl brought the shining clothing out of her chamber, / and laid it on the polished carriage” (6.74–75; [[phaeinos]]). ἐΰξεστος = εὖ (“well”) + ξέω (“scrape, plane”). LSJ glosses it “well-planed, well-polished, of carpenters' work”, and its examples include the wagon of Priam in Iliad 24. Priam's mule wagon and Nausicaa's are described with the same word.

The wagon has several epithets in the book. It is “high, with good wheels” (6.58, 6.70; [[well-rounded]]) and “well-wheeled” (6.72; [[well-wheeled]]), and later “fine” (καλῆς ἐπʼ ἀπήνης, 6.252). See [[mules]] and [[nausicaa]].
