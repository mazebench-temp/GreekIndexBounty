---
summary: Four uses in Book 6. Athena in disguise calls Alcinous Nausicaa's “famous father”; Nausicaa calls the dirty laundry “glorious clothes”; the travelers reach the “famous grove” of Athena; and Odysseus, in his prayer, calls Poseidon “the famous earth-shaker”.
---

**The famous father (6.36).** Athena speaks to Nausicaa in a dream in the likeness of Dymas' daughter (6.22; [[daughter-of-dymas]]) and tells her: “urge your famous father in the early dawn” to have the mules and wagon made ready. The phrase πατέρα κλυτόν elsewhere in the Odyssey names Agamemnon (1.300, 3.198, 3.308). Here it names [[alcinous]].

**The glorious clothes (6.58).** Nausicaa asks her father for the wagon “so that I may take the glorious clothes / to the river to wash, which lie dirty” (6.58–59). The clothes are κλυτά and dirty in the same breath. A scholion on the line explains the epithet as a mark of what the clothes are by nature, not what they are at that moment (οὐ τὰ τότε, ἀλλὰ τὰ φύσει). It compares the “shining moon” of Il. 8.555 (schol. 6.58, Dindorf p. 299). See [[nausicaa]].

**The famous grove (6.321).** At sunset the travelers reach “the famous grove, / holy to Athena” (6.321–322; [[grove-of-athena]], [[hieros]]). Nausicaa had called it ἀγλαὸν ἄλσος Ἀθήνης, “a splendid grove of Athena” (6.291).

**The famous earth-shaker (6.326).** In his prayer in the grove Odysseus reminds Athena of the time “when the famous shaker of the earth shattered me” (6.326; [[shaker-of-the-earth]], [[poseidon]]). He used the same phrase in Book 5, when he was still swimming off Scheria: “I know how the famous earth-shaker is angry with me” (5.423). Odysseus complains of Poseidon's violence, but he keeps the god's honorific epithet. See [[atrytone]] and [[the-wrath-of-poseidon]].
