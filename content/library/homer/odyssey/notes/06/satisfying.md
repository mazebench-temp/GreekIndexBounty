---
summary: The queen packs “satisfying food of every kind” in a chest for her daughter's outing (6.76).
grc: [μενοεικέʼ]
---

“And her mother put in a chest satisfying food / of every kind, and put in relishes, and poured in wine / in a goatskin” (6.76–78). She also gives olive oil in a golden flask for the girls to anoint themselves (6.79–80). LSJ explains μενοεικής as “suited to the desires, satisfying”, mostly of meat and drink, and cites this line. See [[queen-arete]] and [[food-and-drink]].

The same word marks the provisions of another journey. Calypso promises Odysseus food, water and wine μενοεικέα to keep off hunger (5.165–166), and she puts in “many satisfying relishes” when he sails (5.267). Both women pack food for a journey of Odysseus' story.
