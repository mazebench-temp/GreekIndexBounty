---
summary: Athena goes into Nausicaa's “intricately worked chamber” (θάλαμον πολυδαίδαλον, 6.15) to bring her the dream.
---

“She went her way into the intricately worked chamber, in which a girl / was sleeping, like the immortal goddesses in build and in looks” (6.15–16). The room has two handmaids at the doorposts and shining doors (6.18–19; [[phaeinos]], [[handmaids-of-nausicaa]]). In the Iliad πολυδαίδαλος describes objects, such as a corselet. The other Odyssey uses are objects too: gold among the gifts of the Phaeacians (13.11) and a necklace brought for Eurymachus to give to Penelope (18.295). Here it describes a room.

The epithet is the first sign of the wealth of the house that Odysseus will see in Book 7 (7.84–132). See [[house-of-alcinous]], [[nausicaa]] and [[like-the-goddesses]].
