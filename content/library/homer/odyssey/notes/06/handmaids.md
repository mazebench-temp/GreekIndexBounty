summary: Book 6 has two households of serving women. The queen's handmaids sit with her at the hearth and her slave women sit behind her chair, while the princess's own handmaids go with her to the river and do most of the book's work. The same girls are called both ἀμφίπολοι and δμῳαί.
heading: The women of the house
passages:
  - 6.52-53 | The queen with her handmaid women
  - 6.305-307 | Her slave women sit behind her
---

**Two households.** The serving women of Book 6 belong to the mother and to the daughter. The queen sits at the hearth “with her handmaid women” (σὺν ἀμφιπόλοισι γυναιξίν, 6.52), and in Nausicaa's picture of the hall “her slave women sit behind her” (δμωαὶ δέ οἱ εἵατʼ ὄπισθεν, 6.307; see [[queen-arete]]). The princess has her own: two at her door, others who go with her to the river, wash, play, and care for the stranger (6.18–19, 6.84–117, 6.198–248; see [[handmaids-of-nausicaa]]). The men of the house are the δμῶες, who make the wagon ready at the king's order (6.69–73; see [[doulios]]).

**Two words for the same girls.** The narrator and the speakers call Nausicaa's companions ἀμφίπολοι, “handmaids”, sixteen times. Once the narrator calls them δμῳαί, “slave women”: “when they had had their fill of food, the slave women and she herself” (6.99). The words overlap here as they do elsewhere: ἀμφίπολος names the woman by her service to a mistress, δμῳή by her status. A scholion on their beauty, which comes “from the Graces” (6.18), remarks that it is not what one expects of slave bodies (schol. P on 6.18, Dindorf p. 295).

**The phrase ἀμφίπολοι γυναῖκες.** The handmaids of a great house are “handmaid women” (6.52, 6.80). The phrase returns in Book 7, where Arete made the clothes she recognizes “with her handmaid women” (7.235), and Alcinous blames his daughter for not bringing the stranger home with them (7.300).

**The spelling at 6.307.** Murray's text prints δμῳαί with the iota subscript at 6.99 and δμωαὶ without it at 6.307. LSJ notes that the manuscripts write both δμῳή and δμωή (s.v. δμῳή).
