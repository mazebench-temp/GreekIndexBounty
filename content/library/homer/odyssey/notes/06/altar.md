---
summary: Odysseus once saw a young palm springing up beside the altar of Apollo on Delos.
---

“Once on Delos, beside the altar of Apollo, I saw such a thing, a young sapling of a palm tree springing up” (Δήλῳ δή ποτε τοῖον Ἀπόλλωνος παρὰ βωμῷ, 6.162–163; see [[palm-tree]], [[delos]], [[apollo]] and [[the-palm-shoot-on-delos]]). The altar places the palm in the god's precinct and gives Odysseus' praise of Nausicaa its note of awe. In Book 7 βωμός has its other sense, a raised base: golden youths holding torches stand “on well-built bases” in the house of Alcinous (7.100; LSJ s.v. βωμός).
