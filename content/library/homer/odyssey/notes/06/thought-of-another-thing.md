---
summary: The formula turns the plot twice in Book 6. At 6.112 Athena devises the meeting just as the girls are ready to go home; at 6.251 Nausicaa, after the meeting, takes up the same preparations and devises the way into the city.
passages:
  - 6.110-117 | Athena's other thought
  - 6.251-255 | Nausicaa's other thought
---

**Athena's thought (6.112).** The washing, the meal and the ball game are over, and Nausicaa is “about to go home again, after yoking the mules and folding the fine clothes” (6.110–111). If the party leaves, Odysseus sleeps on and the meeting does not happen. “Then in turn the gray-eyed goddess Athena thought of something else: / that Odysseus should wake and see the fair-faced girl, / who would lead him to the city of the Phaeacian men” (6.112–114). The ὡς-clause gives the content of the thought, and the next verses carry it out without naming the goddess again: the princess throws the ball at a handmaid, misses, and it falls into a deep eddy; the girls shout, and Odysseus wakes (6.115–117; see [[ball-play]]). This is Athena's second plan in the book. The first was the dream that brought the girl to the river (6.13–40; see [[dream-scene]] and [[athena]]).

**Nausicaa's thought (6.251).** After Odysseus has eaten, the same verb is used of the girl: αὐτὰρ Ναυσικάα λευκώλενος ἄλλʼ ἐνόησεν, “but white-armed Nausicaa thought of other things” (see [[nausicaa]] and [[white-armed]]). The verse is a variant of the Athena line, with the subject at the beginning and the formula at the end. What she does next is the action that 6.110–111 announced and the ball game interrupted. She folds the clothes, puts them on the wagon and yokes the mules (6.252–253; see [[mules]]), and then she tells Odysseus how to come to the city: follow the wagon through the fields, wait in Athena's grove, and go to the palace alone (6.255–315). The thought this time is a plan of conduct for the stranger, and it is hers.

**The pair.** The two uses frame the meeting. The first gives the initiative to the goddess, the second to the girl Athena has guided.
