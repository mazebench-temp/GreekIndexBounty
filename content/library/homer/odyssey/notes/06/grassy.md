---
summary: Odysseus wakes to girls' voices and wonders whether they are nymphs, who hold mountain peaks, springs of rivers “and the grassy meadows” (6.124).
grc: [ποιήεντα]
---

Odysseus hears the shout of the girls and asks himself whose land he has come to. The cry sounds to him like that “of nymphs, who hold the steep peaks of the mountains / and the springs of rivers and the grassy meadows” (6.123–124). See [[nymphs]] and [[odysseus]].

The second verse is identical with Iliad 20.9, where all the nymphs come to Zeus' assembly before the battle of the gods. The narrator has just compared the girls at play to the nymphs of Artemis (6.105–106; [[artemis-among-her-nymphs]]). Odysseus' first guess repeats the narrator's comparison. See also [[with-grassy-meadows]].
