summary: Three uses in forty verses. Nausicaa twice gives the same whole-verse order to her handmaids, “give the stranger food and drink” (6.209, 6.246), and the narrator reports the order carried out in the same words (6.248). The first order is set aside for the bath; the second is obeyed at once.
passages:
  - 6.207-210 | The first order
  - 6.246-250 | The order repeated and obeyed
---

**The first order (6.209).** Nausicaa has heard Odysseus' plea and calls back her frightened handmaids. She gives the reason for receiving him, “from Zeus are all strangers and beggars, and a small gift is a dear one” (6.207–208; see [[strangers-and-beggars-are-from-zeus]] and [[xenia]]), and then the order in a whole verse: ἀλλὰ δότʼ, ἀμφίπολοι, ξείνῳ βρῶσίν τε πόσιν τε, “but, handmaids, give the stranger food and drink” (see [[handmaids]]). A second order follows: “and bathe him in the river” (6.210). The handmaids carry out the second order first. They lead him to a sheltered place, lay out clothes and the flask of oil, and tell him to bathe (6.211–216), and he asks to bathe alone (6.218–222; see [[bathing]]).

**The repeated order (6.246).** After the bath and Athena's beautification Nausicaa speaks to the handmaids again (6.239–246). She says that he has come by the will of the gods, that he now looks like a god, and she wishes that “such a man might be called my husband” (πόσις, 6.244). Then she ends with the verse of 6.209, word for word. In her speech, πόσις, “husband”, stands two verses above πόσιν, “drink”; the two nouns are declined alike and differ only in meaning (see [[posis]]). The repeated verse returns her from her wish to her duty as hostess.

**The order carried out (6.248).** “So she spoke, and they listened closely to her and obeyed, / and set food and drink beside Odysseus” (6.247–248). The narrative repeats the words of the order, as Homer often reports a command carried out in the words of the command. Then: “He, much-enduring brilliant Odysseus, drank and ate / greedily; for he had long been without a taste of food” (ἐδητύος … ἄπαστος, 6.249–250; see [[much-enduring]]). It is his first meal since the storm broke up his raft (5.313–332).
