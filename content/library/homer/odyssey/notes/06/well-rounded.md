summary: Nausicaa asks for a “high wagon with good wheels” (6.58), and her father grants it in the same words (6.70). In the Iliad εὔκυκλος describes shields; these are its only Odyssey uses, and LSJ renders it “well-wheeled” here.
grc: [ἐΰκυκλον]
en: [with good wheels]
---

The phrase is ἀπήνην / ὑψηλὴν ἐύκυκλον, “a carriage, / high, with good wheels”. The ἀπήνη is a four-wheeled wagon drawn by mules (LSJ s.v. ἀπήνη). Nausicaa asks for it at the start of her request (6.57–58). Alcinous grants it at the end of his answer, “the slaves will make ready for you a carriage, / high, with good wheels, fitted with an upper frame” (6.69–70). He repeats her words and adds a detail. The ὑπερτερίη is “the upper part or body of a carriage, opposed to the axle and wheels” (LSJ). The father adds the box to the wheels the daughter asked for. See [[nausicaa]], [[alcinous]] and [[mules]].

{{quote:ede9e502-b1ee-51b3-814d-ef6b1d20ce45}}

LSJ divides the word's uses by object. In the Iliad it always describes the round shield, “well-rounded” ([[shield]]). In the Odyssey it describes the wagon, “well-wheeled”, and the Odyssey has no other use. When the slaves prepare it, the narrator gives the wagon a second wheel epithet, ἄμαξαν ἐύτροχον ἡμιονείην, “the well-wheeled mule wagon” (6.72; [[well-wheeled]]).
