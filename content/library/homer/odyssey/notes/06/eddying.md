---
summary: The girls drive the unyoked mules “along the eddying river” to graze (6.89). Later the ball falls into one of the river's deep eddies.
grc: [δινήεντα]
---

“And drove them along the eddying river / to graze on the honey-sweet field grass” (6.89–90; [[sweet-as-honey]]). δινήεις is from δίνη, “eddy, whirlpool”. The river's eddies matter to the plot: when Nausicaa throws the ball to a handmaid, she misses and the ball falls “into a deep eddy” (βαθείῃ δίνῃ, 6.116). The girls cry out, and the cry wakes Odysseus (6.117). See [[river-of-scheria]] and [[mules]].

The only other Odyssey use is of the river Enipeus, at whose mouths Poseidon lay with Tyro (11.242).
