---
summary: When Athena leaves Nausicaa's bedside, the narrator follows her to Olympus and gives the only description in Homer of the gods' home as a place untouched by weather, framed by her departure (6.41, 6.47).
refs: [6.41-47]
---

{{quote:Od. 6.41-47 | Olympus}}

**The frame.** The description is a ring. “So saying, gray-eyed Athena went away / to Olympus” (6.41–42) opens it, and “There the gray-eyed one went, when she had made all clear to the girl” (6.47) closes it, with the same verb ἀπέβη and the same epithet (see [[ring-composition]] and [[gray-eyed]]). Between the two the narrator leaves the story for five verses. Then the story resumes with dawn and the girl's waking (6.48; see [[dawn-line]]).

**Against the storm.** The verses come just after the storm of Book 5, in which Poseidon gathered the clouds, stirred the sea and let loose all the winds, and Odysseus was beaten by wind and wave for two days and nights (5.291–296, 5.388–390). The seat of the gods is defined by the absence of those things: “It is not shaken by winds, nor ever by rain / is it wet, nor does snow come near it” (6.43–44). The man the goddess is helping sleeps under two bushes grown from one root, a wild olive and an olive, through which neither the force of the winds nor the sun's rays nor the rain (ὄμβρος) could pass (5.476–482). The narrator describes that mortal shelter in the same negative terms and with two of the same words, “winds” and “rain” (ἀνέμων 5.478, ὄμβρος 5.480; ἀνέμοισι, ὄμβρῳ 6.43). This contrast is a reader's inference; the text does not draw it.

**“They say.”** The narrator speaks of the gods' home as something reported, ὅθι φασί (6.42), though he has just followed a goddess there. The scholia and the modern commentators disagree about why (see the entry).

**The gods' delight.** “In it the blessed gods take their delight all their days” (τέρπονται … ἤματα πάντα, 6.46; see [[olympians]]). The verb of their delight is the verb of Artemis “delighting in wild boars and swift deer” (τερπομένη, 6.104; see [[artemis-among-her-nymphs]]).
