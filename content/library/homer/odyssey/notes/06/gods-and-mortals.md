---
summary: Book 6 measures its mortals against the gods. Nausicaa sleeps “like the immortal goddesses”, Odysseus asks whether she is a goddess or a mortal, Nausicaa says that the beautified stranger is “like the gods”, and Alcinous drinks his wine “like an immortal”.
refs: [6.16, 6.149-153, 6.203, 6.243, 6.309]
---

The book applies the comparison with the gods to each of its main figures.

- **Nausicaa.** The narrator introduces her asleep, “like the immortal goddesses in build and in looks” (6.16; see [[like-the-goddesses]]). Odysseus opens his plea with the question “are you some goddess, or a mortal?” and likens her to Artemis (6.149–153; see [[god-or-mortal]] and [[artemis]]).
- **Odysseus.** After Athena's work Nausicaa says that he, who seemed unsightly before, “now is like the gods who hold the wide sky” (6.243; see [[divine-beautification]]).
- **The Phaeacians.** Nausicaa calls her people “very dear to the immortals” (6.203), and the founder Nausithous is θεοειδής, “godlike” (6.7; see [[phaeacians]] and [[godlike]]).
- **Alcinous.** In Nausicaa's picture of the hall her father “sits and drinks his wine like an immortal” (ἀθάνατος ὥς, 6.309; see [[alcinous]]).

The question “god or mortal?” is answered each time for the mortal. The real gods in the book stay unseen or disguised (see [[seeing-the-gods]]).
