---
summary: Nausicaa and her handmaids throw off their veils, κρήδεμνα, to play ball by the river.
grc: [κρήδεμνα]
en: [veils]
---

When the clothes are drying and the meal is over, the girls “played at ball, having thrown off their veils” (σφαίρῃ ταὶ δʼ ἄρʼ ἔπαιζον, ἀπὸ κρήδεμνα βαλοῦσαι, 6.100; see [[ball-play]]). κρήδεμνον is a woman's headdress or veil. LSJ derives it from κράς, “head”, and δέω, “bind”. The translation renders it “veils” (see the translation notes on 6.100). A woman of rank wears it among men: Penelope holds her shining veil before her cheeks when she comes down to the suitors (1.334). Away from the city, with no men present, the girls put it off for the game. The verb is the one used when Andromache casts her veil from her head in grief (Iliad 22.468–470).

The same word ends the previous book. The sea-goddess Ino gave Odysseus her immortal κρήδεμνον to tie under his chest so that he could not drown, and once he reached land he let it go into the river, where Ino took it back (5.346–351, 5.373, 5.459–462).
