---
summary: Athena visits Nausicaa's dream in the likeness of “the daughter of Dymas, famous for ships” (6.22), and the epithet gives the father a Phaeacian kind of fame.
---

Athena stands over the sleeping girl and speaks “in the likeness of the daughter of Dymas, famous for ships, / who was of the same age as she, and was dear to her heart” (6.22–23). The father is named only to identify the daughter, and his epithet is his only description. See [[daughter-of-dymas]], [[dymas-the-phaeacian]] and [[dream-scene]].

{{quote:33f62be4-1749-5282-8541-c2509340bf46}}

The fame of Dymas is the fame of his people. Book 6 shows the Phaeacians as a nation of ships. Nausicaa tells Odysseus that bows and quivers mean nothing to them, “but for masts and the oars of ships and balanced ships, / glorying in which they cross the gray sea” (6.270–272; [[balanced-ships]], [[gray-sea]]). In the same book the ships line the road to the city, and the men tend their tackle by the Posideion (6.264–269; [[posideion]]). Several Phaeacian names are formed with the word for ship, among them Nausithous (6.7; [[nausithous]]) and Nausicaa herself. In later books the Odyssey calls the Phaeacians ναυσίκλυτοι, “famous for ships” (for example 8.191). See [[phaeacians]].
