---
summary: Book 6 uses Homer's ordinary words for slaves, not δούλη or δούλιος. The king's male slaves, δμῶες, make the wagon ready at his order, and the women who serve the queen and the princess are called δμῳαί as well as ἀμφίπολοι.
heading: The slaves of the palace
grc: [δμῶες, δμώεσσιν]
en: [slaves]
passages:
  - 6.69-73 | The slaves make the wagon ready
---

**The men.** Alcinous grants his daughter the wagon and adds, “the slaves will make ready for you a carriage” (δμῶες ἐφοπλίσσουσιν, 6.69). “So saying, he called to the slaves, and they obeyed” (δμώεσσιν ἐκέκλετο, τοὶ δʼ ἐπίθοντο, 6.71). They bring the mule wagon out, lead up the mules and yoke them (6.72–73; see [[wagon]] and [[mules]]). δμώς, from the root of δαμάω, “subdue”, is properly a slave taken in war, and then any slave; the dative plural δμώεσσι is cited by LSJ from this verse (LSJ s.v. δμώς).

**A reading of Aristophanes.** At 6.74–75 the text has the girl bring the clothing from her chamber (φέρεν) and lay it on the wagon (κατέθηκεν). A scholion reports that Aristophanes of Byzantium read the plurals φέρον and κατέθηκαν, so that the slaves did the carrying (schol. H.P. on 6.74, Dindorf p. 300; see [[aristophanes-of-byzantium]]). Another scholion glosses δμώεσσιν at 6.71 with θεραπαινίσιν, “maidservants” (schol. V, Dindorf p. 300).

**The women.** The female slaves of the house are δμῳαί. The word is used once of Nausicaa's companions at the river (6.99) and once of the women who sit behind the queen (6.307; see [[handmaids]] and [[handmaids-of-nausicaa]]). The noun δούλη and the adjective δούλιος do not occur in Book 6.
