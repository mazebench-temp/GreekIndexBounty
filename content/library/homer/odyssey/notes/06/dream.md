---
summary: The personified Dream of Iliad 2 has no part in Book 6. The ὄνειρον that Nausicaa wonders at (6.49) is the common noun, the dream that Athena brought her.
except: [6.49]
---

At dawn Nausicaa “wondered at the dream” (ἀπεθαύμασʼ ὄνειρον, {{ref:Od. 6.49}}). The word is the common noun, treated under [[onar]]. No dream-god is sent here. [[athena]] comes to the girl's bed herself, “like a breath of wind”, stands above her head in the likeness of the daughter of Dymas, speaks, and goes back to Olympus (6.20–41; see [[dream-scene]]). In Iliad 2 the sender stays on Olympus and the Dream is the messenger. In Odyssey 6 the goddess is her own messenger, and the poet calls the experience a dream only when the girl wakes.
