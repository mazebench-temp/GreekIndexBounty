---
summary: Two passages of Book 6 are linked by the word. Artemis is “easily known” among her nymphs (6.108), and the house of Alcinous is “easily known” among the houses of the Phaeacians (6.300).
---

**Artemis (6.108).** In the simile for Nausicaa among her maids, Artemis holds her head and brow above all her nymphs, “and she is easily known, though all are beautiful” (ῥεῖά τʼ ἀριγνώτη πέλεται, καλαὶ δέ τε πᾶσαι, 6.107–108). The point of the comparison follows: “so she stood out among her handmaids, the unwed maiden” (6.109). A scholion reports that the texts of Aristarchus' school and almost all others read ῥεῖά τε here (schol. 6.108, Dindorf about p. 303). See [[artemis-among-her-nymphs]] and [[nausicaa]].

**The house (6.300).** Nausicaa tells Odysseus to ask in the city for the house of her father: “It is easily known, and even a child could lead you, / an infant” (ῥεῖα δʼ ἀρίγνωτʼ ἐστί, 6.300–301). The reason follows: “for in no way are they built like it, / the houses of the Phaeacians, such as is the house of Alcinous / the hero” (6.301–303). See [[house-of-alcinous]] and [[alcinous]].

The two passages have the same structure. One figure stands out among many of the same kind, the goddess among her nymphs and the king's house among the houses. The word is the same, and the half-verse opens with ῥεῖα both times. The daughter is recognized as the Artemis among girls, and her father's house as the first among houses.
