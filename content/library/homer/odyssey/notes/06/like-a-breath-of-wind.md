{{quote:Od. 6.15-21 | Like a breath of wind}}

**The closed room.** The narrator has described the room before the goddess enters it. Nausicaa sleeps in “the intricately worked chamber”, two handmaids are beside her, one by each of the doorposts, and “the shining doors were closed” (6.15–19; see [[visit-scene]] and [[handmaids]]). Merry and Riddell take ἐπέκειντο as “were closed”, that is, they lay to against the doorposts (on 6.19). Nothing is said of a door opening. The simile replaces the entry: “and she, like a breath of wind, rushed to the girl's bed” (6.20).

**What follows.** The next verse is the formula of the dream-figure: “and stood above her head and spoke to her” (6.21; see [[dream-scene]]). Then the goddess takes the likeness of a friend, the daughter of Dymas (6.22–24; see [[daughter-of-dymas]]). The simile gives the goddess no form while she moves, and the disguise gives her a human form when she speaks. Nausicaa knows the visit only as a dream when she wakes (6.49).

**The winds of Book 5.** About 130 verses earlier, Athena acted on real winds. In the storm she bound the paths of the other winds and roused the north wind to carry Odysseus toward the Phaeacians (5.382–387). In Book 6 she moves like a wind herself, into the house of the people to whom she has brought him.

**Her departure.** When the dream speech is over, the narrator does not use the wind again. Athena simply “went away to Olympus” (6.41), and the description of the gods' seat follows (6.42–47; see [[seat-firm-forever]]).
