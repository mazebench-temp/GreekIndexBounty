**The golden flask.** The queen sends her daughter to the river with “liquid olive oil in a golden flask” (χρυσέῃ ἐν ληκύθῳ, 6.79; see [[olive-oil]] and [[queen-arete]]). The verse returns when the handmaids give Odysseus oil in a golden flask for his bath (6.215; see [[bathing]]). It is presumably the flask that the queen gave. A golden flask for everyday oil marks the wealth of the house, which Odysseus will see in Book 7, with its golden doors, silver doorposts and lintel, and the gold and silver dogs made by Hephaestus (7.88–94).

**Gold around silver.** When Odysseus has bathed and dressed, Athena “poured grace upon him” as a skilled man pours gold around silver (χρυσὸν περιχεύεται ἀργύρῳ, 6.232). The craftsman has been taught “craft of every kind” by Hephaestus and Pallas Athena, and he completes graceful works (6.233–234). The simile is treated in [[gold-poured-on-silver]]; the scene in [[divine-beautification]]. The verses return when Odysseus is bathed in his own house before Penelope (23.159–162).
