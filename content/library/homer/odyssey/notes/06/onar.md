---
summary: Nausicaa wakes and “wonders at the dream” that Athena brought her. She sets out to tell her parents, but she does not report it.
passages:
  - 6.48-51 | She wondered at the dream
---

At dawn Nausicaa wakes:

{{quote:b531fcc7-b61b-557b-88e3-144b631983cd}}

The noun is ὄνειρος, the common word for a dream (see the article above). The verb ἀπεθαύμασʼ, from ἀποθαυμάζω, “marvel much at”, occurs only here in Homer. LSJ cites this line first and then Herodotus. The dream is the visit of [[athena]] in the likeness of the daughter of Dymas (6.20–41). The narrator tells it as a goddess's visit, and the girl wakes from it as from a dream (see [[dream]] and [[dream-scene]]).

Nausicaa goes through the house “to tell her parents” (ἵνʼ ἀγγείλειε τοκεῦσιν, 6.50). She does not tell them the dream. She asks her father for the wagon and gives other reasons: his own clothes for the council, and her brothers' clothes for the dance (6.57–65). The narrator explains: “she felt shame to name the marriage of her prime to her dear father” (6.66; see [[aidos]]). The marriage was the dream's subject (6.25–35). Alcinous “understood everything” and gave her the wagon (6.67–70).
