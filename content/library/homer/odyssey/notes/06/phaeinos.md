---
summary: Three things in Nausicaa's world are “shining” in Book 6. The doors of her chamber (6.19), the clothes she carries out to wash (6.74), and the whip with which she drives the mules home (6.316).
grc: [φαειναί]
---

**The doors (6.19).** Athena comes to the chamber where Nausicaa sleeps with two handmaids at the doorposts, “and the shining doors were closed” (θύραι δʼ ἐπέκειντο φαειναί). The goddess passes the closed doors “like a breath of wind” (6.20; [[like-a-breath-of-wind]]). The shining doors belong to a rich house: the chamber is [[intricately-worked]] (6.15). See [[house-of-alcinous]].

**The clothes (6.74).** “The girl brought the shining clothing out of her chamber” (ἐσθῆτα φαεινήν). These clothes are on their way to the river because they are dirty (6.59). The epithet names their lasting quality, as κλυτά, “glorious”, does at 6.58 ([[klytos]]).

**The whip (6.316).** At the end of her last speech she “lashed with the shining whip / the mules” (6.316–317). The text gives no detail about the whip. See [[nausicaa]] and [[mules]].

The three uses frame Nausicaa's day: they mark the chamber at night, the start of the outing, and the drive home.
