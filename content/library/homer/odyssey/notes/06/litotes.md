---
summary: Nausicaa tells her handmaids that the stranger has come “not against the will of all the gods who hold Olympus” (6.240). The double negative means that some god has brought him.
refs: [6.240]
---

After Athena has made Odysseus beautiful, Nausicaa speaks to her handmaids. “Not against the will of all the gods who hold Olympus / does this man mingle with the godlike Phaeacians” (οὐ πάντων ἀέκητι θεῶν, 6.240–241). The words deny the opposite of what she means. A scholion gives the positive sense: “but clearly there is some god who brought Odysseus safely here” (schol. Q.T. on 6.240, ed. Dindorf). The understatement lets her claim divine favor for the stranger without naming a god. The reader knows that the god is Athena (6.229–235; see [[divine-beautification]] and [[athena]]). The next lines turn to his looks and to her wish for such a husband (6.242–245; see [[nausicaas-wish-244-245]]).
