**Setting.** At sunset they reach “the famous grove, / holy to Athena”, where Nausicaa has told him to wait, and Odysseus sits down and “at once he prayed to the daughter of great Zeus” (6.321–323; see [[grove-of-athena]]). The place is the goddess' own, and the occasion is the threshold of the city. No gesture is told.

**Invocation.** κλῦθί μευ, αἰγιόχοιο Διὸς τέκος, Ἀτρυτώνη, “Hear me, child of aegis-bearing Zeus, Atrytone” (6.324; see [[hear-me]]). The whole verse is the opening of Diomedes' prayer to Athena in the Iliad (Iliad 5.115) and of Penelope's prayer to her in Odyssey 4.762; Odysseus' own prayer in the night raid begins with the same first half (Iliad 10.278).

**The claim, reversed.** Where the pattern puts the claim, “if ever I …”, Odysseus puts a complaint. “Now at least listen to me, since before you never listened / when I was being shattered, when the famous shaker of the earth shattered me” (6.325–326). Diomedes had reminded the goddess that she once stood by his father (Iliad 5.116–117). Odysseus reminds her that she did not stand by him. In the Iliad his own prayer to her had named the opposite claim: “you who always stand by me in all my toils” (Iliad 10.278–279). ῥαιομένου … ἔρραιε, “being shattered … shattered”, repeats the verb of the wreck (see [[poseidon]] and [[the-wrath-of-poseidon]]).

**Request.** “Grant that I may come to the Phaeacians as one dear to them and pitied” (6.327). The verse is Priam's request to Zeus before he goes to Achilles' hut, with “the Phaeacians” in place of “Achilles” (Iliad 24.309; see [[dear-and-pitied]]).

**Answer.** “So he spoke in prayer, and Pallas Athena heard him” (6.328), the verse that closes Nestor's prayer to Athena at Pylos (3.385). Then the narrator qualifies the answer at once: “But she did not yet appear to him face to face; for she stood in awe of / her father's brother; and he raged furiously / against godlike Odysseus until he reached his own land” (6.329–331; see [[aidos]]). The reproach of 6.325–326 is thus answered by the narrator, not by the goddess: she kept away because of Poseidon. Her help comes in the next book, in disguise, as a girl with a pitcher who leads him through the city in a mist (7.14–42).

The scene keeps the parts of the type-scene and changes their content. The claim is a reproach, and the answer is hearing without appearance. See [[prayer]].
