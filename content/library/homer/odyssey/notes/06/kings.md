---
summary: The kings of the Phaeacians appear once in Book 6, as the “famous kings” whom Alcinous goes to meet in council at dawn. Book 8 counts them: twelve kings rule the people, and Alcinous is the thirteenth.
heading: The Phaeacian council
passages:
  - 6.53-55 | Alcinous goes to the council
---

When Nausicaa comes to ask for the wagon, she meets her father at the door, “as he went to join the famous kings / in council, to which the noble Phaeacians were calling him” (μετὰ κλειτοὺς βασιλῆας / ἐς βουλήν, 6.54–55; see [[famous]] and [[assembly]]). The verse gives the Phaeacians a council of kings, and a king who is summoned to it by the people's leaders. A scholion explains μετά as “behind” or “to” the kings, and remarks that here the poet calls the notable men “kings” (schol. E on 6.54, Dindorf p. 298; see [[basileus]]).

Nausicaa's request uses the same setting. It is fitting, she tells her father, that he, “among the foremost”, should deliberate in the council with clean clothes (μετὰ πρώτοισιν ἐόντα / βουλὰς βουλεύειν, 6.60–61; see [[alcinous]]). The best men of the people are also her suitors, “the best men throughout the land … of all the Phaeacians” (ἀριστῆες, 6.34–35), and she promises Odysseus that in her father's house he will come to know “all the Phaeacians who are the best” (6.257; see [[phaeacians]]).

The other books fill in the picture. Athena tells Odysseus that he will find “kings nurtured by Zeus” feasting in the palace (7.49), the leaders of the Phaeacians sit there drinking and eating (7.98–99), and Alcinous tells the “leaders and rulers of the Phaeacians” that twelve eminent kings rule among the people, with himself as the thirteenth, and that each should give the stranger a cloak, a tunic and a talent of gold (8.387–393).
