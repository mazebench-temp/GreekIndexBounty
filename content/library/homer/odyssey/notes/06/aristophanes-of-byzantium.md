---
summary: The scholia on Book 6 report four readings of Aristophanes, all small matters of wording. Murray's text agrees with his φάτις at 6.29 and follows none of the others.
---

From the scholia (ed. Dindorf, vol. 1; page numbers in brackets):

- **6.29.** Athena says that a good report rises among men from fine clothes. Callistratus read χάρις, “joy”, for φάτις, “report”, and said that Aristophanes had changed the word to φάτις (mss. H.P., p. 296). See [[phemis]].
- **6.74–75.** The base text says that “the girl brought the shining clothing out of her chamber” and laid it on the wagon. Aristophanes wrote the plurals φέρον and κατέθηκαν, so that the servants, who have just yoked the mules (6.71–73), bring and load the clothes (mss. H.P., p. 300). See [[chariot-journey]].
- **6.264.** The harbor has a narrow εἰσίθμη, “entrance”. The scholion says that the word has a grave accent like στάθμη, and that Aristophanes wrote it with σ, ἴσθμη, from ἰσθμός, “isthmus, neck of land” (mss. B.H.Q., p. 315). Monro and Allen give his reading as εἰσίσθμη. See [[epistion]].
- **6.297.** For δώματʼ ἀφῖχθαι, “reached the house”, Aristophanes read δώματα ἶχθαι, with the simple verb (ms. H, p. 318; the form is given as in the apparatus of Monro and Allen).
