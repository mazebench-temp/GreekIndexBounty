summary: Book 6 uses the “finding” of the visit scene three times. Athena enters the house of Alcinous and finds Nausicaa asleep in her chamber with two handmaids at the door; Nausicaa finds her mother at the hearth and her father on his way out; and she tells Odysseus in advance whom he will find in the hall, and at what occupation.

**Athena at the chamber (6.13–21).** The first two parts of the scene, the arrival and the finding, are told in full. Athena goes “to his house” (τοῦ μὲν ἔβη πρὸς δῶμα, 6.13), then into “the intricately worked chamber”, where the narrator shows what the visitor finds: the girl asleep, “like the immortal goddesses in build and in looks”, two handmaids beside her, one by each doorpost, and the shining doors closed (6.15–19; see [[nausicaa]], [[handmaids]] and [[intricately-worked]]). The house and the person at an occupation are the usual matter of the finding; here the occupation is sleep, and the attendants are guards at a shut door. The reception cannot follow, because no one wakes to receive her. The scene turns at 6.20 into the [[dream-scene]]: the visitor passes the closed doors like a breath of wind and stands above the sleeper's head.

**Nausicaa finds her parents (6.50–55).** At dawn the girl goes through the house to tell her parents, “and she found them indoors” (κιχήσατο δʼ ἔνδον ἐόντας, 6.51). The verses give each at an occupation, as the finding does: her mother at the hearth with her handmaid women, “turning the sea-purple wool on her distaff”; her father on his way out to the council of the kings (6.52–55; see [[queen-arete]], [[alcinous]] and [[distaff]]). She stands close to him and speaks (6.56).

**What Odysseus will find (6.303–309).** Nausicaa's directions describe a finding before it happens. When the house and courtyard enclose him, he is to pass quickly through the hall until he reaches her mother: “she sits at the hearth in the light of the fire, / turning the sea-purple wool on her distaff, a wonder to see, / leaning against a pillar; and her slave women sit behind her”, and her father's throne leans against the same pillar, where he sits and drinks his wine “like an immortal” (6.303–309). The verse of the queen's occupation is the verse of 6.53, so the picture of the hall at dawn is repeated for the hall at evening. The visit itself, with the supplication of the queen that Nausicaa plans here (6.310–315; see [[supplication-type-scene]]), comes in the next book (7.136–154).
