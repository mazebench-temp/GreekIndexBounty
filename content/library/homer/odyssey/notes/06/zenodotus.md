---
summary: Three readings of Zenodotus in Book 6 survive in the scholia, all of them small matters of form, and the scholia judge two of them wrong. One of them, ἐμεῖο at 6.290, is said to have prevailed in the copies.
---

From the scholia (ed. Dindorf, vol. 1; page numbers in brackets):

- **6.1.** The scholion in mss. H and P reads “Zenodotus, καθεῦδε”, an accent on the verb “slept” (p. 293). Dindorf's note, following Düntzer, holds that the scholion originally gave Zenodotus' reading as ἐκάθευδε, with the augment. Zenodotus, he says, liked to add the augment where the meter allowed, while Aristarchus left it out. Murray prints καθεῦδε.
- **6.137.** For σμερδαλέος, “terrible”, of Odysseus as the girls see him, some read λευγαλέος, “wretched”, and Zenodotus ἀργαλέος, “grievous”. The scholion in mss. H and P calls both readings wrong (p. 305). See [[the-lion-and-the-girls]].
- **6.256.** A note in mss. H and Q says that all copies have πατρὸς ἐμοῦ, not ἐμεῦ, and adds that in some places Zenodotus changes such forms for the worse (p. 314).
- **6.290.** Nausicaa's πατρὸς ἐμοῖο, “my father”. A note in mss. H and Q says that ἐμοῖο, from the adjective ἐμός, is the better writing, because ἐμεῖο comes from the pronoun ἐμεῦ. “Zenodotus wrote ἐμεῖο, and it prevailed.” Dindorf cites scholia on Iliad 14.118 and 24.486, which note the same habit of Zenodotus, πατρὸς ἐμεῖο for πατρὸς ἐμοῖο (p. 317 with notes). Monro and Allen record ἐμεῖο as Zenodotus' reading. Murray prints ἐμοῖο.

See [[aristarchus]].
