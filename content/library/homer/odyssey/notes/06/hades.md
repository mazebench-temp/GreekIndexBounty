---
summary: Hades is named once in Book 6. Nausithous, who led the Phaeacians to Scheria, “had already been overcome by fate and had gone to Hades”.
grc: [Ἄϊδόσδε]
---

ἀλλʼ ὁ μὲν ἤδη κηρὶ δαμεὶς Ἄϊδόσδε βεβήκει, “but he had already been overcome by fate and had gone to Hades” ({{ref:Od. 6.11}}). The line closes the short history of the Phaeacians (6.4–10) and hands the rule to [[alcinous]] (6.12; see [[nausithous]] and [[ker-doom]]). Ἄϊδόσδε is the genitive Ἄϊδος with the suffix -δε of direction, “to the house of Hades”. Murray's Odyssey prints it as one word. The Iliad text of this library prints the same form as two words, Ἄϊδος δέ ({{ref:Iliad 7.330}}).
