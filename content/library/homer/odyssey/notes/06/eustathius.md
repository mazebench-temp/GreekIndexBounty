---
summary: In Book 6 Eustathius gives the book's traditional title, tells the story of Sophocles playing Nausicaa's ball game, reports Dionysodorus' reading πάλλαν, and keeps the lines of Nausicaa's wish that Aristarchus rejected.
---

From the commentary on the Odyssey, ed. Stallbaum, vol. 1:

- **The title.** “Book Zeta is entitled ‘the arrival of Odysseus among the Phaeacians’”. He reads the book as showing how women help Odysseus: Circe and Calypso, Athena, Helen at Troy, and now Nausicaa (p. 234). See [[title-of-odyssey-6]].
- **The ball game.** He praises the brevity with which the poet wakes Odysseus. He lists later devotees of ball play, among them Sophocles, who “they say” won acclaim acting Nausicaa playing ball in the *Plyntriai* (p. 241). See [[sophocles-nausicaa]].
- **6.115.** Dionysodorus wrote πάλλαν for σφαῖραν. Eustathius asks whether the game is the one called ἐφετίνδα, in which the ball is shown to one player and thrown to another (p. 242). See [[athenaeus-ball-games]].
- **6.242–245.** He paraphrases δέατʼ as ἐδόκει, “seemed”, and keeps Nausicaa's wish, glossing ἅδοι as ἀρέσκοι, “might please” (p. 251). See [[deato-or-doato]] and [[nausicaas-wish-244-245]].
- **6.265.** He explains ἐπίστιον as an Ionic form, without aspiration, and compares Herodotus (p. 253). See [[epistion]].
