---
summary: The Phaeacians once lived “in Hypereia with its wide dancing floors” (6.4), the home that they left because of the Cyclopes.
grc: [εὐρυχόρῳ]
---

The book's account of the Phaeacians begins with their first home: they “once in former days lived in Hypereia with its wide dancing floors, / near the Cyclopes, overweening men, / who kept doing them harm, and were greater in strength” (6.4–6). Nausithous then led them to Scheria (6.7–8). See [[hypereia-of-the-phaeacians]], [[cyclopes]] and [[nausithous]].

εὐρύχορος is a standing epithet of cities and lands. LSJ notes that it meant “with broad dancing places” at first and then became a conventional epithet. The Odyssey gives it to Elis (4.635), Iolcus (11.256), Thebes (11.265) and Lacedaemon (13.414, 15.1). The form εὐρυχόρῳ has two endings and so goes with the feminine name Ὑπερείῃ.

The epithet goes with a city that the Phaeacians abandoned. Dancing itself has a place in Book 6. Nausicaa's brothers “always want to wear freshly washed clothes / when they go to the dance” (6.64–65), and Odysseus pictures her family watching “such a young shoot going into the dance” (6.157). See [[dance]] and [[phaeacians]].
