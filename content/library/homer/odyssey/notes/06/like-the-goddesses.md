Athena enters the chamber “in which a girl / was sleeping, like the immortal goddesses in build and in looks” (6.15–16). The words differ from ἐϊκυῖα θεῇσι of the entry, but the idea is the same. The verse names two aspects of beauty, φυή (“build, stature”) and εἶδος (“looks”; [[eidos]]). See [[nausicaa]].

The comparison prepares the rest of the book. Her two handmaids have “beauty from the Graces” (6.18). In the Artemis simile she stands out among her maids as the goddess does among her nymphs (6.102–109). Odysseus asks whether she is a goddess or a mortal and compares her to Artemis “in looks and stature and build” (6.149–152; [[god-or-mortal]]).
