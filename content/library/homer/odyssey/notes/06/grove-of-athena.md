---
summary: The grove is the last stage of Odysseus' approach to the city. Nausicaa tells him to wait there so that the townspeople will not see them together, and at sunset he sits there and prays to Athena, who hears him but does not appear.
refs: [6.292-295, 6.322]
---

**Nausicaa's plan.** Nausicaa does not want to be seen entering the city with a handsome stranger, for fear of gossip (6.273–288). So she gives Odysseus a place to wait. He is to sit in the grove of Athena until he thinks the girls have reached the house, and then walk into the city alone (6.291–299; see [[house-of-alcinous]]). The grove lies between the farmland and the city, on Alcinous' own land and within a shout of the walls (6.293–294). It is close enough to the city for the plan, and far enough for the two to arrive separately.

There are two readings of 6.291 in the scholia: δήεις, “you will find”, which Murray prints and which “the more elegant copies” read, and δήομεν, “we will find”. In the second reading Nausicaa includes herself in the arrival at the grove.

**Arrival at sunset.** “And the sun set, and they came to the famous grove, holy to Athena, where brilliant Odysseus sat down” (6.321–322; see [[sunset-lines]]). Scholia (P.Q.T.) take δύσετο as “was setting”, because Athena later pours a mist around Odysseus as he walks into the city, and so it must still be day when he enters. The grove is ἱρόν, “holy” (see [[hieros]]). It is the place where Odysseus can address the goddess in her own precinct.

**The prayer.** Odysseus prays to Athena at once (6.323–327; see [[prayer]] and [[athena]]). Athena hears him “but did not yet appear to him face to face”, because she respected Poseidon (6.328–331; see [[poseidon]]). Book 7 begins in the grove: “So brilliant Odysseus prayed there, much-enduring, but the strength of the two mules carried the girl to the town” (7.1–2).
