---
summary: Cattle appear in Book 6 only in the lion simile, as the first prey of the hungry lion.
---

The mountain lion to which Odysseus is compared “goes among the cattle or the sheep or after the wild deer” (αὐτὰρ ὁ βουσὶ μετέρχεται ἢ ὀίεσσιν / ἠὲ μετʼ ἀγροτέρας ἐλάφους, 6.132–133; see [[the-lion-and-the-girls]], [[sheep]] and [[deer]]). The words come from the simile of Sarpedon at the wall (Iliad 12.299–301; see [[the-mountain-lion-and-the-fold]]).
