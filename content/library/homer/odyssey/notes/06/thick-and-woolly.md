---
summary: Athena makes thick, curling hair fall from Odysseus' head, “like the hyacinth flower” (6.231). οὖλος here describes hair, not wool.
grc: ["=οὔλας"]
en: [curling]
---

After his bath Athena makes Odysseus taller and stronger, “and down from his head / she let fall curling locks, like the hyacinth flower” (6.230–231; [[like-the-hyacinth-flower]], [[gold-poured-on-silver]]). The verse returns unchanged when Athena makes him handsome before Penelope (23.158). See [[hair]] and [[athena]].

οὖλος in this sense is “thick, woolly, curly”. The Odyssey uses it of thick woolen cloaks (χλαίνας οὔλας, 4.50, 17.89). Merry and Riddell explain οὖλαι κόμαι as the thick hair of a vigorous man and contrast the bald old man of 18.354. They report two derivations: Buttmann from εἰλέω (“squeeze”), and Curtius from the root of ἔριον (“wool”), Latin *vellus* and English “wool” (note on 6.231, pp. 271–272). They also quote a commentator who refers the comparison with the hyacinth to the thick, curling hair and not to its color, and who notes that Eustathius already gave this explanation (πρὸς τὸ οὖλον, “to its curliness”).

The accent marks the word: the pattern is accent-sensitive, because οὐλάς (with the accent on the last syllable) is the barley grain of sacrifice (3.441).
