---
summary: Nausithous, the founder of the Phaeacian city, is “godlike” (θεοειδής) when he leads his people from Hypereia to Scheria (6.7).
---

“From there godlike Nausithous made them rise up and led them away, / and settled them in Scheria, far from grain-eating men” (6.7–8). The narrator lists his acts as a founder: he drove a wall around the city, built houses, made temples of the gods and divided the plowlands (6.9–10). Then the narrator says that he is dead and that Alcinous rules (6.11–12). See [[nausithous]], [[scheria]] and [[alcinous]].

θεοειδής = θεός (“god”) + εἶδος (“form, looks”): “godlike in looks”, a synonym of the entry's θεοείκελος. In the Odyssey it is a common epithet of young men of rank: Telemachus (for example 1.113), Theoclymenus (15.271) and the suitors Eurymachus (4.628) and Antinous (21.277). Book 7 gives it to Alcinous (7.231) and tells that Nausithous was a son of Poseidon (7.56–62). Here the epithet goes with the founder. See [[eidos]].
