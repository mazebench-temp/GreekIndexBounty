---
summary: Nausicaa ends her directions with the hope that Odysseus will reach “your well-built house and your native land” (6.315). The phrase has σήν, “your”, in place of φίλην, “dear”.
---

“If she is minded in friendship toward you in her heart, / then there is hope for you to see your dear ones and to reach / your well-built house and your native land” (6.313–315). “She” is Nausicaa's mother, whom Odysseus must supplicate (6.310–312; [[queen-arete]]). See [[nostos]] and [[day-of-homecoming]].

{{quote:4c806587-0533-5876-b19f-5e102c3b1ec6}}

The three verses return in Book 7, when Athena gives Odysseus the same advice about the queen (7.75–77). There the house is ὑψόροφον, “high-roofed”, instead of ἐυκτίμενον, “well-built”. The phrase οἶκον ἐυκτίμενον καὶ σὴν ἐς πατρίδα γαῖαν also closes the prophecy of Proteus to Menelaus (4.476) and occurs at 15.129 and 23.259.
