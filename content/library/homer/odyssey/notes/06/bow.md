---
summary: Nausicaa says that the Phaeacians do not care for the bow or the quiver. Her guest is an archer.
---

“For the Phaeacians do not care for the bow or the quiver, but for masts and the oars of ships” (οὐ γὰρ Φαιήκεσσι μέλει βιὸς οὐδὲ φαρέτρη, 6.270–271; see [[quiver]], [[ships]] and [[phaeacians]]). βιός is the rare word for the bow; it differs only by accent from βίος, “life”, so the index matches it by accent. The line places the Phaeacians apart from warriors (compare 6.201–205, where Nausicaa says that no enemy comes to their land). The stranger she is guiding is one of the best archers alive. At the Phaeacian games in Book 8 he boasts that he knows how to handle the polished bow, and that only Philoctetes surpassed him at Troy (8.215–222).
