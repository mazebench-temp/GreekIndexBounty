---
summary: The first use of the verse. After the river bath Athena gives Odysseus thick curling hair “like the hyacinth flower”, between making him taller and pouring grace on him like a gilder.
refs: [6.231]
---

**Its place.** The verse is the second of three gifts that Athena gives Odysseus after his bath: size and build (6.229–230), hair (6.231), and grace on head and shoulders (6.232–235; see [[gold-poured-on-silver]]). It follows directly on his washing. Five verses earlier he scrubbed from his head “the scurf of the barren sea” (ἁλὸς χνόον, 6.226), and now the goddess lets hair fall from that head (κὰδ δὲ κάρητος, 6.230). The salt-crusted head of the castaway becomes a head of thick curls.

**The hair of the girls and of the man.** Hair has marked the girls in this scene. Odysseus is ashamed to strip naked among “girls with lovely tresses” (κούρῃσιν ἐυπλοκάμοισι, 6.222), and the narrator uses the same epithet of the handmaids when Nausicaa speaks to them after she sees him (6.238; see [[with-lovely-tresses]]). Earlier the girls threw off their veils to play (6.100). Now the stranger has hair to match.

**Who sees it.** The beautification is told for its effect on Nausicaa: “the girl gazed at him” (6.237), and she says that he now looks like the gods (6.242–243; see [[nausicaa]]). The verse does not say whether the hair is dark or curled (see the entry above). Merry and Riddell note that the main clause of the long sentence begins at 6.229, after the clauses of the bath (on 6.229).
