---
summary: The Book 6 scholia (Dindorf vol. 1, pp. 293–320) are the source for the book's ancient textual criticism: two athetesis notices, the readings of Zenodotus, Aristophanes, Callistratus, Rhianus and Aristarchus, and ancient explanations of Scheria, the palm of Delos and the lions of Europe.
---

- **Atheteses.** Aristarchus athetized Nausicaa's wish, 6.244–245, but hesitated over 6.244 because Alcman had adapted it (p. 314): [[nausicaas-wish-244-245]]. An unnamed critic athetized the fourteen lines of imagined gossip, 6.275–288, as unsuited to the speaker (p. 317): [[the-gossip-lines-275-288]].
- **Readings of the critics.** [[aristarchus]], [[zenodotus]] and [[aristophanes-of-byzantium]] have Book 6 notes. Callistratus read δυερός for διερός at 6.201 (p. 311; see [[dieros]]). Rhianus read αἰθήρ for αἴθρη at 6.44 and τῇ ἔνι for τῷ ἔνι at 6.46 (p. 297; see [[lucretius-seats-of-the-gods]]).
- **Scheria and Corcyra.** The scholia on 6.3, 6.8, 6.195 and 6.204 argue for and against the identification (pp. 294, 310–311): [[corcyra-and-the-phaeacians]].
- **The palm of Delos.** Mss. E and V make the palm Leto's; mss. B, P and Q deny it (p. 307): [[the-palm-of-delos]].
- **The lions.** A note on 6.103 cites Aristotle for the absence of lions on Taygetus and Erymanthus (pp. 302–303): [[aristotle-lions-of-europe]].
- **The nymphs.** A note on 6.123 explains why Odysseus thinks of nymphs (p. 304): [[the-nymphs-cry-122-124]].
- **Words.** δέατʼ at 6.242 (p. 314): [[deato-or-doato]]. ἐπίστιον at 6.265 (pp. 315–316): [[epistion]].
- **The book.** The two prose summaries of Book 6 (p. 293) and the book titles printed with the scholia (pp. 1–2): [[title-of-odyssey-6]].
