---
summary: Pausanias records two ancient works of art that showed scenes of Book 6, and he cites the Delian palm of Odysseus' supplication.
---

- **Polygnotus' painting.** In the building with paintings on the left of the Propylaea at Athens, Polygnotus painted “by the river, Odysseus coming upon the girls who were washing with Nausicaa, in the same way as Homer composed it” (1.22.6). See [[nausicaa-in-greek-art]] and [[polygnotus]].
- **The chest of Cypselus.** On the cedar chest in the temple of Hera at Olympia, girls on a mule cart, one with the reins and one veiled, were thought to be Nausicaa and her maidservant driving to the washing places (5.19.9). See [[nausicaa-in-greek-art]].
- **The palm.** Most games give a palm to the victor. The custom began, they say, with Theseus' games on Delos, “and Homer too made mention of the palm on Delos, in Odysseus' supplication to the daughter of Alcinous” (8.48.2–3; see 6.162–163). See [[the-palm-of-delos]].
