---
summary: In her directions to the city Nausicaa describes the harbor. Each Phaeacian has his own berth for his ship along the road, and this is why the ships lie there.
passages:
  - 6.262-269 | The harbor and the ships
---

Nausicaa tells Odysseus what he will see when they reach the city:

{{quote:Od. 6.262-265 | Each man his own berth}}

The clause with ἐπίστιον gives a reason (γάρ). Merry and Riddell explain it: the existence of a berth for every ship is the reason why the ships are drawn up along the roadway (note on 6.265, p. 276). The scholia add that the entrance is narrow because so many ships are drawn up there (schol. E.T.V. on 6.265, Dindorf p. 316). A scholion on 6.264 says that each Phaeacian owns a ship in which he “lodges” (ἐναυλίζεται; Dindorf p. 315, read in the OCR text only).

The picture continues with the place of assembly around the shrine of Poseidon (6.266–267; see [[posideion]]), where the men tend the tackle, cables and sails, and taper the oars (6.268–269). Then comes the reason for all of it: the Phaeacians care for masts, oars and ships, and not for the bow (6.270–272; see [[phaeacians]] and [[pompe]]).

The berths are part of a city with walls, two harbors and a narrow entrance (6.262–264). Merry and Riddell picture a road sloping down to the water with the ships hauled up on slips along it (note on 6.265, p. 276).
