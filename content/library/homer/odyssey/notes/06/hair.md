---
summary: Athena makes curling locks fall from Odysseus' head, like the hyacinth flower, after his bath.
grc: [κόμας]
---

When Odysseus has bathed and dressed, Athena makes him taller and sturdier, “and down from his head she let fall curling locks, like the hyacinth flower” (κὰδ δὲ κάρητος / οὔλας ἧκε κόμας, ὑακινθίνῳ ἄνθει ὁμοίας, 6.230–231; see [[divine-beautification]], [[like-the-hyacinth-flower]] and [[thick-and-woolly]]). The same lines return when Athena beautifies him again before Penelope (23.157–158). Hair is the first sign of the change, before the grace poured on his head and shoulders (6.235; see [[gold-poured-on-silver]]). The girls on the shore are ἐυπλόκαμοι, “with lovely tresses” (6.135, 6.198, 6.222, 6.238; see [[with-lovely-tresses]]).
