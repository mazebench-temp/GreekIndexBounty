---
summary: Hypereia is named once, in the opening history of the Phaeacians. They lived there first, near the Cyclopes, until Nausithous led them away to Scheria.
---

When Athena goes to the Phaeacian city, the narrator stops to tell who the Phaeacians are (6.3–12). The history begins in Hypereia: the Phaeacians “once in former days lived in Hypereia with its wide dancing floors” (6.4). It ends with the present: Nausithous is dead and Alcinous rules (6.11–12; see [[the-phaeacians-leave-hypereia]], [[nausithous]] and [[alcinous]]).

The Phaeacians left Hypereia because stronger neighbors kept doing them harm (6.5–7; see [[exile-and-refuge]]). Their neighbors were the Cyclopes. Odysseus is lying asleep on the shore of Scheria at this moment, and he has already met the Cyclopes. In Book 9 he will tell the Phaeacians how he blinded the Cyclops Polyphemus. That Cyclops is a son of Poseidon, and his blinding is the reason for Poseidon's anger against Odysseus (1.68–75; see [[cyclopes]] and [[poseidon]]). So the old enemies of the Phaeacians are tied to the anger that has brought Odysseus to their shore.

Book 7 adds a second link. The Phaeacian kings descend from Poseidon through Nausithous (7.56–62), and Alcinous says that the gods show themselves openly to the Phaeacians, “since we are near to them, as are the Cyclopes and the wild tribes of the Giants” (7.205–206). Homer does not say whether ἐγγύθεν, “near”, means near in place or near in descent.
