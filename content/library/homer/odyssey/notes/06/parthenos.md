**The end of maidenhood.** Athena, in the likeness of a girl of Nausicaa's age, gives the reason for the washing: “your marriage is near” (6.27), and she must have fine clothes for herself and for those who will lead her home. She ends the speech (6.33–35):

{{quote:fc2d8497-1890-5462-ba79-668139ec4dc4}}

οὔ τοι ἔτι δὴν παρθένος ἔσσεαι, “you will not be a maiden much longer”. The status is defined by its end. The best men of all the Phaeacians are already courting her (μνῶνται, 6.34), as the imagined townsman will later complain that she dishonors “these men here … who are courting her, many and good” (6.283–284; see [[marriage]]). Her brothers are also of an age to marry: two are married and three are ἠίθεοι, “unmarried young men in their bloom” (6.62–63), the masculine partner of παρθένος.

**παρθένος ἀδμής.** The narrator twice calls her παρθένος ἀδμής, “the unwed maiden” (6.109, 6.228), and the phrase occurs nowhere else in Homer. ἀδμής is “untamed”, the word for a heifer or a mule never put under the yoke (*Odyssey* 3.383, 4.637; see [[unbroken]]). The first use closes the simile of Artemis among her nymphs on the mountains, taller than all of them and easily known, “though all are beautiful” (6.102–109; see [[artemis]]). The simile places Nausicaa among her handmaids as the goddess stands among the nymphs, and the closing phrase names what she shares with them: she is unmarried. The second use comes when Odysseus, bathed, puts on “the clothes that the unwed maiden had given him” (6.228). The clothes she was to wash for her marriage now dress the stranger.

**Maiden and stranger.** The book is built on what an unmarried girl may and may not do. She drives the wagon to the river and plays with her handmaids (6.81–101). She stays when the naked man comes out of the thicket, because Athena gives her courage (6.139–140). She will not be seen entering the city with him, and she is indignant with a girl who “mingles with men before she has come to a public marriage” (6.286–288; see [[nemesis]] and [[phemis]]). Odysseus, for his part, will not touch the knees of “the girl” (κούρη, 6.147; see [[gounata]]) and will not strip before girls (6.221–222; see [[aidos]]).
