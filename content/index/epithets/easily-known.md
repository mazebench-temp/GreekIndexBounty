## Meaning

ἀρίγνωτος = ἀρι- (a prefix that strengthens, “very”) + γνωτός (“known”, from γιγνώσκω, “recognize”). LSJ: “easy to be known”. In the Odyssey it has a feminine form, ἀριγνώτη (6.108). In a bad sense it means “notorious”: the suitor Antinous calls Eumaeus ὦ ἀρίγνωτε συβῶτα, “you notorious swineherd” (17.375).

## Use

The word usually goes with ῥεῖα, “easily”: a thing is “easily known”.

- **Gods.** Poseidon has spoken to the two Ajaxes in the shape of Calchas, and the lesser Ajax recognizes the god from behind as he goes: “gods are easily known” (Il. 13.72).
- **A family.** Menelaus says to Pisistratus that “easily known is the offspring of a man for whom Zeus spins good fortune” (4.207).
- **A goddess among nymphs.** Artemis among her nymphs (6.108; [[artemis-among-her-nymphs]]).
- **A house.** The house of Alcinous (6.300; [[house-of-alcinous]]). Odysseus says the same of his own house on Ithaca: it is “easily known, even among many” (17.265).
