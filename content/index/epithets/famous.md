## Meaning

κλειτός is a verbal adjective from the family of κλέος (“fame, report”; [[kleos]]): “famous, renowned.” It is the standing epithet of the Trojans' allies, κλειτοὶ ἐπίκουροι (for example Il. 6.227), with fuller forms such as τηλεκλειτοί, “far-famed” (Il. 6.111).

κλυτός is the verbal adjective of κλύω (“hear”): what is heard of, “famous, renowned” (LSJ: “renowned, glorious”). It has the same root as Sanskrit *śrutá-* and Latin *inclutus*. The masculine form can go with a feminine name: κλυτὸς Ἱπποδάμεια (Il. 2.742), κλυτὸς Ἀμφιτρίτη (Od. 5.422).

Some compounds of κλυτός are separate epithets: [[famed-for-his-craft]] (κλυτοτέχνης, Hephaestus), [[of-the-famous-horses]] (κλυτόπωλος, Hades), [[famed-for-his-spear]] (δουρικλυτός), and ναυσίκλυτος, “famous for ships”, which the Odyssey gives to the Phaeacians (Od. 7.39, 8.191) and the Phoenicians (Od. 15.415).

## Use

{{quote:f33d8b09-db86-5ab5-9ca6-26eb273ec9dc}}

After Aphrodite has carried Paris off, Menelaus ranges the field looking for him. No one can show him where Paris is, and no one would have hidden him out of love: “he was hateful to them all, like black doom” ([[black-doom]], [[like-black-doom]]). The famous allies came to fight for the man they now will not shelter. See [[trojan-allies]].

LSJ arranges the uses of κλυτός in three groups, and Homer has all three.

- **Gods and persons.** κλυτὸς ἐννοσίγαιος, “the famous earth-shaker”, is Poseidon (Il. 9.362; Od. 5.423, 6.326; [[shaker-of-the-earth]]). In the Odyssey πατέρα κλυτόν, “his famous father”, is Agamemnon, whom Aegisthus killed (Od. 1.300, 3.198, 3.308).
- **Places and buildings.** Houses (κλυτὰ δώματα, Il. 2.854; Od. 10.60), a harbor (Od. 10.87, 15.472), and Athena's grove on Scheria (Od. 6.321).
- **Things made with skill.** Armor (κλυτὰ τεύχεα, Il. 5.435; Od. 12.228), clothes (Od. 6.58), and the works that Athena teaches women to make (κλυτὰ ἔργα, Od. 20.72).

A name can be κλυτόν too. When Odysseus tells the Cyclops his “famous name”, the name he gives is Nobody (Od. 9.364).
