## Meaning

μελίφρων = μέλι (“honey”) + φρήν (“mind, heart”): “honey to the heart,” gladdening the mind. Homer gives it to wine (οἶνον μελίφρονα, as in Il. 6.264, where Hector refuses the cup his mother offers), to grain (Il. 8.188), and once to sleep. It belongs with the other words for sleep in Book 2: [[sweet-sleep]] (νήδυμος, γλυκύς) and [[ambrosial]] (ἀμβρόσιος).

## Use

The only sleep that Homer calls μελίφρων ends the Dream's message:

The warning not to forget is needless. Agamemnon wakes with the message fixed in his mind and acts on it at once ({{ref:Iliad 2.35-47}}). What he should fear is not forgetting but believing. The sweetness of sleep is the Dream's last kindness to a man it is deceiving.

## μελιηδής

μελιηδής = μέλι (“honey”) + ἡδύς (“sweet”). LSJ: “honey-sweet”. The translation renders both μελιηδής and μελίφρων “honey-sweet”.

- **Wine.** μελιηδέα οἶνον, “honey-sweet wine”, is the usual phrase (for example Il. 6.258, 10.579, 12.320; Od. 3.46, 9.208, 14.78, 18.426). See [[wine]].
- **Food for animals and men.** The horses of Diomedes eat “honey-sweet wheat” at their manger (Il. 10.568–569). Nausicaa's mules graze on “honey-sweet field grass” (Od. 6.90). The lotus has “honey-sweet fruit” (Od. 9.94).
- **Wax.** Circe tells Odysseus to knead “honey-sweet wax” and stop his companions' ears against the Sirens (Od. 12.47–48).
- **Transferred senses.** Life is “honey-sweet” when it is taken away (μελιηδέα θυμόν): from a Thracian killed in his sleep (Il. 10.495), and from Odysseus' mother, who died of longing for him (Od. 11.203). Tiresias tells Odysseus that he seeks a “honey-sweet homecoming” (Od. 11.100). Penelope says that “honey-sweet sleep” has left her (Od. 19.551).
