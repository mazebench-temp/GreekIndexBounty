## Meaning

κλυτός is the verbal adjective of κλύω (“hear”): what is heard of, “famous, renowned” (LSJ: “renowned, glorious”). It belongs to the family of κλέος, “fame” ([[kleos]]), which also gives the epithet [[famous]] (κλειτός). It has the same root as Sanskrit *śrutá-* and Latin *inclutus*. The masculine form can go with a feminine name: κλυτὸς Ἱπποδάμεια (Il. 2.742), κλυτὸς Ἀμφιτρίτη (5.422).

Compounds made with it are separate epithets: [[famed-for-his-craft]] (κλυτοτέχνης, Hephaestus), [[of-the-famous-horses]] (κλυτόπωλος, Hades), [[famed-for-his-spear]] (δουρικλυτός), and ναυσίκλυτος, “famous for ships”, which the Odyssey gives to the Phaeacians (7.39, 8.191) and the Phoenicians (15.415).

## Use

LSJ arranges its uses in three groups, and Homer has all three.

- **Gods and persons.** κλυτὸς ἐννοσίγαιος, “the famous earth-shaker”, is Poseidon (Il. 9.362; 5.423, 6.326; [[shaker-of-the-earth]]). In the Odyssey πατέρα κλυτόν, “his famous father”, is Agamemnon, whom Aegisthus killed (1.300, 3.198, 3.308).
- **Places and buildings.** Houses (κλυτὰ δώματα, Il. 2.854; 10.60), a harbor (10.87, 15.472), and Athena's grove on Scheria (6.321).
- **Things made with skill.** Armor (κλυτὰ τεύχεα, Il. 5.435; 12.228), clothes (6.58), and the works that Athena teaches women to make (κλυτὰ ἔργα, 20.72).

A name can be κλυτόν too. When Odysseus tells the Cyclops his “famous name”, the name he gives is Nobody (9.364).
