## Meaning

ἐϋῶπις = εὖ (“well”) + ὤψ (“eye, face”), with the feminine ending -ις. LSJ gives both senses, “fair-eyed, or fair to look on”, because ὤψ can mean the eye or the face. The word has the same form as two epithets of goddesses: [[gray-eyed]] (γλαυκῶπις), of Athena, and [[ox-eyed]] (βοῶπις), of Hera.

## Use

In Homer the word occurs only in the accusative phrase ἐυώπιδα κούρην, “the fair-faced girl”, twice in Odyssey 6 (6.113, 6.142). LSJ cites later uses of the same kind: a girl in the Homeric Hymn to Demeter (333), and the Moon in Pindar (*Olympian* 10.74). See [[nausicaa]].
