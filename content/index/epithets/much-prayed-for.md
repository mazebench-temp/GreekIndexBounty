## Meaning

πολυάρητος = πολύς (“much”) + ἀράομαι (“pray”; ἀρά, “prayer”). LSJ: “much-wished-for, much-desired”. A scholion on Od. 6.280 gives two explanations: a god “who hears many prayers”, or one “deemed worthy of many prayers”, and it derives the word from πολύ and ἀρά, “prayer” (schol. B, Dindorf about p. 317). See [[prayer]].

Compare [[thrice-prayed-for]] (τρίλλιστος), another epithet made from the language of prayer.

## Use

The word occurs twice in Homer, both times in the Odyssey.

- In Nausicaa's imagined gossip of the townspeople, the stranger might be “some much-prayed-for god” who has come down to her “at her prayer” (6.280).
- When the infant Odysseus is put on the knees of his grandfather Autolycus, the nurse Euryclea asks him to name “your child's dear child”, because “he is much prayed for by you” (19.403–404). Autolycus then gives him the name Odysseus (19.409).

The Homeric Hymn to Demeter uses it of a late-born son (220), as LSJ cites.
