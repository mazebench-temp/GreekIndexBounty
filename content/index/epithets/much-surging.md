## Meaning

πολύκλυστος = πολύς (“much”) + κλύζω (“wash, dash against”). LSJ gives two senses. In the active sense the sea is “much-dashing, stormy”. In the passive sense a place is “washed by many a wave”, as Cyprus is in Hesiod (*Theogony* 199).

## Use

In Homer the word occurs only in the Odyssey, always at the end of the verse in the phrase πολυκλύστῳ ἐνὶ πόντῳ, “in the much-surging sea”.

- Menelaus tells of the island of Pharos, which lies “in the much-surging sea” off Egypt (4.354).
- Nausicaa says that the Phaeacians live “far apart in the much-surging sea” (6.204).
- Odysseus, in his false tale to Penelope, says that his companions “all perished in the much-surging sea” (19.277).

Hesiod uses the same phrase when Cronus throws the genitals of Ouranos into the sea (*Theogony* 189). See [[sea]].
