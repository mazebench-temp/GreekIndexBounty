## Descriptions

Nausicaa says that the house is “easily known” (ῥεῖα δʼ ἀρίγνωτʼ), and that even a child could lead a stranger to it, because the houses of the other Phaeacians are not built like it (6.300–303). The narrator gives the full description when Odysseus stands at its threshold (7.81–132):

- It shines “like the gleam of the sun or of the moon” (7.84–85).
- The walls are bronze from the threshold to the inner end, with a frieze of blue κύανος (7.86–87).
- The doors are gold, the doorposts and lintel silver on a bronze threshold, and the handle is gold (7.88–90).
- Gold and silver dogs, made by Hephaestus, guard the house. They are immortal and ageless (7.91–94).
- Seats line the walls, covered with fine woven cloths, where the leaders of the Phaeacians eat and drink (7.95–99). Golden youths on pedestals hold torches to light the feast at night (7.100–102).
- Fifty slave women grind grain, weave and spin (7.103–111).
- Outside the court lies an orchard of four measures, whose fruit never fails in winter or summer, with a vineyard, garden beds and two springs (7.112–131).

“Such were the splendid gifts of the gods in the house of Alcinous” (7.132).

The hall has a hearth, a pillar, and the seats of the king and queen beside it (6.305–309). Odysseus walks through the hall to the queen, clasps her knees, and then sits down on the hearth in the ashes (7.139–154; see [[queen-arete]] and [[supplication]]).
