## Descriptions

{{quote:1e500a02-6d6f-52e1-b2e5-687293e1de63}}

- ἀγλαὸν ἄλσος, “a splendid grove” (6.291), and κλυτὸν ἄλσος ἱρὸν Ἀθηναίης, “the famous grove, holy to Athena” (6.321–322).
- ἄλσος is a grove, and in particular a sacred grove. LSJ cites this passage for the sacred sense. The word can also mean a sacred precinct without trees (LSJ s.v. ἄλσος).
- αἰγείρων, “of poplars” (6.292). The αἴγειρος is the black poplar (LSJ). Merry and Riddell take the genitive as describing what the grove consists of.
- ἐν δὲ κρήνη νάει, ἀμφὶ δὲ λειμών: “in it a spring flows, and around it is a meadow” (6.292).
- The grove is “near the road” (ἄγχι κελεύθου, 6.291). A scholion in manuscript H has the lemma ἄγχι θαλάσσης, “near the sea”, and notes κελεύθου as another reading.
- The royal estate (τέμενος) and a flourishing orchard of Alcinous are there, “as far from the city as a man can make himself heard when he shouts” (6.293–294; see [[temenos]] and [[within-shouting-distance]]).

Poplars, water and a sacred place go together elsewhere in the Odyssey. On Ithaca the town spring stands in a round grove of water-loving poplars, with an altar of the nymphs above it (17.205–211). At the edge of Ocean are the groves of Persephone, with tall poplars and willows (10.509–510).
