## Etymology

Ὑπέρεια (Hypereia), Ionic Ὑπερείη. LSJ explains the name as “High-land” and gives this line as its only use for the Phaeacians' home (LSJ s.v. Ὑπέρεια). Merry and Riddell take Hypereia and Scheria as simple descriptions of the land, “the Highlands” and “the Coast” (Merry and Riddell on 5.34). The same name belongs to a spring in Thessaly in the Iliad (Il. 2.734, 6.457; see [[hypereia]]). A scholion on 6.4 mentions that spring too.

## Descriptions

{{quote:eb0e85e3-fde4-5f9a-8a62-d2d5528e5fb5}}

- ἐν εὐρυχόρῳ Ὑπερείῃ, “in Hypereia with its wide dancing floors” (6.4; see [[with-wide-dancing-floors]]).
- ἀγχοῦ Κυκλώπων ἀνδρῶν ὑπερηνορεόντων, “near the Cyclopes, overweening men” (6.5; see [[overweening]] and [[cyclopes]]). The Cyclopes harmed the Phaeacians again and again, and they were stronger (6.6).

Homer gives no other location. Hypereia was near the Cyclopes, and the Phaeacians left it for Scheria, “far from grain-eating men” (6.8).

**Where was it?** A scholion on 6.4 (B.E.P.Q.V.) reports three ancient views:

- Some identify Hypereia with Camarina in Sicily.
- Others take the name from ὑπέρ, “beyond”: the land beyond the world known to us.
- Others say that it was once an island near the land of the Cyclopes.

All three are later guesses. Homer locates the place only by its neighbors.
