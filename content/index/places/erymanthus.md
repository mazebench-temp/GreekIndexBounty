## Etymology

Ἐρύμανθος (Erymanthos). The origin of the name is unknown.

## Descriptions

- ἢ κατὰ Τηΰγετον περιμήκετον ἢ Ἐρύμανθον, “either along towering Taygetus or Erymanthus” (6.103). Erymanthus has no epithet of its own in the line (see [[taygetus]]).
- In the simile Artemis goes there “delighting in wild boars and swift deer” (6.104; see [[artemis-among-her-nymphs]], [[boar]] and [[deer]]).

The scholia explain the place and its animals:

- “Erymanthus is a mountain of Arcadia, where, by tradition, many boars and deer lived” (B.P.Q. on 6.103).
- “A mountain and river of Arcadia, where the Erymanthian boar was. No lion is born in these places, but many boars and deer” (E.V. on 6.104; see [[aristotle-lions-of-europe]]).

Merry and Riddell describe Erymanthus as a high range on the borders of Arcadia, Achaea and Elis, the haunt of the boar that Heracles killed (Merry and Riddell on 6.103). Erymanthus occurs only here in Homer.

## Outside Homer

**The river.** The river Erymanthus rises on Mount Lampeia, which is said to be sacred to Pan, and Pausanias suggests that Lampeia is a part of Mount Erymanthus. The river flows through Arcadia, with Mount Pholoe on the right and the land of Thelpusa on the left, into the Alpheius (Pausanias 8.24.4). Strabo also names the Erymanthus among the rivers that the Alpheius receives (Strabo 8.3.12), and reports the view of experienced men that it formed the boundary of Arcadia (Strabo 8.3.32).

**The Erymanthian boar.** Pausanias reports the legend that Heracles, at the command of Eurystheus, hunted beside the Erymanthus a boar greater than all others in size and strength. The people of Cumae in Italy said that the boar's tusks were dedicated in their sanctuary of Apollo. Pausanias finds that claim altogether improbable (Pausanias 8.24.5; see [[heracles]]).
