## Etymology

Τηΰγετος (Tēügetos) is the Ionic form of Ταΰγετος (Taygetos). The origin of the name is unknown. Two ancient and modern explanations exist:

- A scholion on 6.103 (B.P.Q.) derives the name from Taygete, daughter of Atlas, who bore Lacedaemon to Zeus. Pausanias tells the same story. Lacedaemon's mother was Taygete, “after whom the mountain was named”, and his father was Zeus, “according to report” (Pausanias 3.1.2; see [[lacedaemon]]).
- Merry and Riddell connect the first part with ταΰς, “great”, and render “the huge mountain” (Merry and Riddell on 6.103).

## Descriptions

- Τηΰγετον περιμήκετον, “towering Taygetus” (6.103). περιμήκετος is a poetic form of περιμήκης, “very tall, very high”. In Homer it describes only a tall fir on Ida and Taygetus (LSJ s.v. περιμήκετος, citing Il. 14.287 and Od. 6.103). A scholion (E.Q.) calls Taygetus “a very high and very great mountain of Laconia”.
- In the simile of 6.102–104 Artemis goes over Taygetus or Erymanthus, “delighting in wild boars and swift deer” (see [[artemis-among-her-nymphs]], [[erymanthus]], [[boar]] and [[deer]]). A scholion explains why Homer names boars and deer and not lions: lions are not born in these places, as Aristotle testifies (see [[aristotle-lions-of-europe]]).

Taygetus occurs only here in Homer. The Iliad's Catalogue of Ships lists the towns of Lacedaemon under Menelaus, but it does not name the mountain (Il. 2.581–590).

## Outside Homer

**The mountain.** Strabo describes Taygetus as a mountain that rises a little way above the sea, high and steep, joining the Arcadian foothills to the north. Sparta and Amyclae lie beneath it (Strabo 8.5.1). He also reports that some peaks of Taygetus broke off in an earthquake, and that a large quarry of costly stone had recently been opened in the mountain (Strabo 8.5.7).

**Hunting and cult.** Pausanias names Taletum, a peak of Taygetus sacred to Helios, where horses are among the sacrifices offered to the Sun. He adds that the whole of Taygetus is a hunting ground for wild goats and boars, and that it has many deer and bears (Pausanias 3.20.4; see [[helios]]). In his account of Arcadia he refers to Homer's mention of Taygetus and Erymanthus in a sentence about hunting, but the text of that sentence is damaged in the manuscripts (Pausanias 8.24.4).
