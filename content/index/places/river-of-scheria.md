## Descriptions

The poet never names the river. He describes it with common epithets:

- καλλίρροος, “fair-flowing”: Odysseus swims along the coast until he reaches “the mouth of the fair-flowing river” (5.441).
- περικαλλής, “very beautiful”: “the very beautiful stream of the river” (6.85).
- δινήεις, “eddying”: the girls drive the mules “along the eddying river” to graze (6.89; see [[eddying]]).
- ἁλιμυρήεις, “flowing into the sea”: Odysseus lets Ino's veil go “into the river that flows into the sea” (5.460).

At its mouth the place is smooth, free of rocks, and sheltered from the wind (5.442–443). The girls find the same shelter there in Book 6 (6.210–212).

## In the Odyssey

**The river god.** Odysseus has been two days and two nights in the sea after Poseidon wrecked his raft. When he reaches the river mouth he prays to its god, whose name he does not know: “Hear me, lord, whoever you are.” A man who comes as a wanderer is to be respected even by the immortals, and he comes as a suppliant to the river's stream and to its knees (5.444–450). The god stops his current, makes the water calm before him, and saves him “into the outflow of the river” (5.451–453). Odysseus gives Ino's veil back to the river, kisses the earth, and sleeps in a thicket near the water (5.459–493; see [[supplication]]).

**The washing place.** The next morning Nausicaa comes to the same river to wash the household's clothes. Here Odysseus wakes, meets her, bathes, and receives clothes (Book 6). When the wagon leaves the river, Odysseus' first stay in Scheria is over (6.317).
