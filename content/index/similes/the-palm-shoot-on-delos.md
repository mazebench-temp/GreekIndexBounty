## The simile

{{quote:Od. 6.160-169 | The palm on Delos}}

**The vehicle.** A memory, not a scene of nature. “Once on Delos, beside the altar of Apollo, I saw such a thing, / a young sapling of a palm tree springing up” (φοίνικος νέον ἔρνος ἀνερχόμενον, 6.162–163; see [[delos]], [[apollo]], [[altar]] and [[palm-tree]]). He adds why he was there: “for I went there too, and a great army followed me, / on that journey on which evil sorrows were to come upon me” (6.164–165; see [[odysseus-at-delos]]). Then the effect of the sight: “I stood amazed in my heart / for a long time, since never yet had such a stem come up out of the earth” (6.166–167). δόρυ is the trunk of the palm here, not a spear (see the translation notes).

**The tenor.** “As I admire you, lady, and stand amazed, and I am terribly afraid / to touch your knees” (ὡς σέ, γύναι, ἄγαμαί τε τέθηπά τε, δείδια δʼ αἰνῶς / γούνων ἅψασθαι, 6.168–169). The comparison is built ὣς δʼ αὔτως … ὡς σέ, “in just the same way … as you” (6.166–168), and it compares two experiences of the speaker: his wonder then and his wonder now. The verb returns in two tenses, ἐτεθήπεα, “I stood amazed” (pluperfect, 6.166), and τέθηπα, “I stand amazed” (perfect, 6.168). The girl is likened to the palm only through what each did to the man who saw it.

**The points of likeness.** The palm is young, slender and upright, unique of its kind, and it grows in a holy place by a god's altar. Each point fits the speech. Odysseus has just called her “such a young shoot” (τοιόνδε θάλος, 6.157), so the plant image is prepared within the same sentence of praise (see [[thrice-blessed]]). He has said that he never saw “such a mortal, neither man nor woman”, and that “awe holds me” (σέβας μʼ ἔχει, 6.160–161; see [[sebas]]); the palm too was such that none like it had come up before. The sacred setting keeps open the possibility he raised first, that she may be a goddess (6.149–152; see [[god-or-mortal]]), and the awe that belongs to a holy thing gives the reason for his distance: he is afraid to touch her (see [[supplication-type-scene]]).

The scholia read it as praise of form. One says that 6.157 is “very emphatic”, as of a man himself amazed. Another, on 6.160, says Odysseus turns to a new resource of praise, the straight and well-grown body, and adds: “we too use the image, calling well-grown people shoots and stems” (schol. P.Q. on 6.157 and H.P.Q. on 6.160, Dindorf p. 307).

**The palm and Leto.** Homer names only a young palm by Apollo's altar. The Hymn to Apollo tells that Leto, in labor on Delos, “threw her arms around the palm” and gave birth (*Homeric Hymn to Apollo* 117). The scholia on 6.163 disagree whether Odysseus means that palm. Mss. E.V. say he means the palm that sprang up for Leto, which she held when she gave birth; mss. B.P.Q. say he does not mean it, and take νέον with ἀνερχόμενον, “newly springing up” (Dindorf p. 307). Merry and Riddell suppose that a palm was always kept in the precinct at Delos, like the sacred olives of the Academy at Athens (note on 6.162, p. 266). See [[the-palm-of-delos]].

## Elsewhere in Homer

The young plant is Homer's image for a young life. Thetis says that Achilles “shot up like a sapling” (ἔρνεϊ ἶσος, Iliad 18.56 = 18.437), and Eumaeus that the gods reared Telemachus “like a sapling” (Odyssey 14.175). Euphorbus falls like an olive sapling, well tended and white with blossom, which a sudden wind tears out of its trench (Iliad 17.53–58). Hecuba calls Hector her “dear young shoot” (φίλον θάλος, Iliad 22.87). The Delian palm is the only one of these images that a speaker presents as something he once saw.

## Outside Homer

Later writers report a palm shown on Delos. Cicero has his brother say, with irony, that perhaps they still show the tall and slender palm that Homer's Ulysses said he saw on Delos (*On the Laws* 1.2), and Pliny reports a palm at Delos shown as dating from the god's own time (*Natural History* 16.240). Pausanias, writing of the palm as a prize at games, recalls that Homer mentions the palm of Delos in Odysseus' supplication to the daughter of Alcinous (8.48.3).
