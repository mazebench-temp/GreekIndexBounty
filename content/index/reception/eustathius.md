summary: Byzantine scholar of the twelfth century, archbishop of Thessalonica, who wrote long commentaries on the Iliad and the Odyssey. They gather the work of ancient grammarians, lexica and scholia, much of it otherwise lost.
aliases: [Eustathius of Thessalonica, Commentarii ad Homeri Odysseam]
---

Eustathius comments on Homer line by line, in a series of notes usually introduced by ὅτι, “(note) that”. He explains words and forms, reports variant readings and ancient opinions, and adds moral and allegorical readings of his own. The standard printed text of his commentary on the Odyssey for this project is the Leipzig edition of G. Stallbaum (vol. 1, 1825), which reproduces the edition printed at Rome, as Stallbaum's preface explains. References give Stallbaum's volume and page.
