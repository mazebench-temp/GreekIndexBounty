*Ulysses* follows Leopold Bloom through Dublin on 16 June 1904. Its eighteen episodes match episodes of the Odyssey, but the published novel gives them only numbers. Their Homeric names, among them “Nausicaa” for episode 13, come from the schemas that Joyce sent to Carlo Linati and that Stuart Gilbert printed in his study of the novel. The novel ends with the places and years of its writing, “Trieste-Zurich-Paris 1914-1921”.

## The episode

Episode 13 takes place at sunset on the rocks of Sandymount Strand, the shore where Stephen Dedalus walked in episode 3. Gerty MacDowell sits with her friends Cissy Caffrey and Edy Boardman, who are minding the twins Tommy and Jacky and a baby. Nearby, a service is being sung in the church of Mary, Star of the Sea. A “gentleman in black” sits apart against a rock. The narrator later names him: “Leopold Bloom (for it is he)”.

The first half of the episode is told in Gerty's idiom, the language of romantic magazine fiction. The second half follows Bloom's thoughts after the girls have gone. As Gerty walks away, Bloom sees that she is lame: “Tight boots? No. She's lame! O!”

## The Homeric pattern

The correspondences are in the action. They are our reading of the episode, set against Book 6:

- **The shore and the girls.** Nausicaa and her handmaids wash and play on the beach at the river mouth (6.85–101). Gerty and her friends sit on the strand with the children.
- **The ball.** Nausicaa's throw misses the handmaid, and the shout wakes Odysseus (6.115–117). In Joyce, one twin kicks the ball toward the rocks. Bloom stops it and throws it back, but it rolls down “right under Gerty's skirt”. Her first kick misses, and the friends laugh. Her second sends it far. Bloom later thinks: “But the ball rolled down to her as if it understood.”
- **The stranger who watches.** Odysseus stands apart and decides to plead from a distance (6.141–147). Bloom stays apart against his rock and never speaks to Gerty.
- **The girl's thoughts of marriage.** Nausicaa is sent to the river by a dream of her wedding, and she wishes for a husband like the stranger (6.25–40, 6.244–245; see [[nausicaas-wish-244-245]]). Gerty dreams of marriage, and her heart goes out to the gentleman as “her dreamhusband”.
- **The gaze.** Each sees the other. Nausicaa gazes at Odysseus, transformed by Athena (6.237). Gerty knows that Bloom is watching her, and she lets him look.

The second half of the episode reverses the Homeric scene. The narrator of Book 6 calls the princess “like the immortal goddesses in build and in looks” (6.16). Bloom's discovery that Gerty is lame brings the romance down to the ordinary.

## Notoriety

The Wikipedia account of the novel reports that the episode attracted “immense notoriety” during the serial publication of the novel, and that it led to prosecution in the United States. The court records were not consulted for this entry.
