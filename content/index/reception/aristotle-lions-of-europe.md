The narrator compares Nausicaa among her handmaids to Artemis among her nymphs. The goddess goes down the mountains, “along lofty Taygetus or Erymanthus, delighting in boars and swift deer” (6.102–104; see [[artemis-among-her-nymphs]]). Taygetus is the mountain range of Laconia, and Erymanthus a mountain of Arcadia.

## The scholia

The scholia explain the choice of animals. One note in mss. B, P and Q says that on Erymanthus, a mountain of Arcadia, “many boars and deer are said to have lived”. Another continues:

> οἱ γὰρ τόποι οὗτοι πλεονάζουσι κάπροις καὶ ἐλάφοις. οὐκ εἶπε δὲ λέουσι, ἐπεὶ οὐ γεννᾶται ἐνταῦθα τὰ ζῷα ταῦτα, ὡς Ἀριστοτέλης μαρτυρεῖ.
>
> “For these places abound in boars and deer. He did not say lions, since these animals are not born there, as Aristotle testifies.” (Dindorf pp. 302–303; the sigla printed after the note are P.Q.)

A note in mss. E and V on 6.104 says the same without naming Aristotle: Erymanthus is a mountain and river of Arcadia, the home of the Erymanthian boar, “and in these places the lion does not occur, but many boars and deer” (Dindorf p. 303). Dindorf's footnote refers the citation to Aristotle, *History of Animals* 6.31 and 8.28, and to Aelian, *On Animals* 3.27.

## Aristotle

Aristotle writes about the lion in two places of the *History of Animals*:

- **6.31.** He rejects as nonsense the story that the lioness expels her womb when she gives birth. The story arose, he says, to explain why lions are few: “for the race of lions is rare and does not occur in many places, but of all Europe only in the region between the Achelous and the Nessus rivers”.
- **8.28.** On the influence of place on animals: “lions are found rather in Europe, and of Europe in the region between the Achelous and the Nessus; leopards in Asia, but in Europe they do not occur.”

The Achelous runs through Acarnania in western Greece, and the Nessus (Nestus) through Thrace past Abdera. Aristotle names no mountains. The scholiast takes his range to exclude Taygetus and Erymanthus, the mountains of Laconia and Arcadia.

Herodotus gives the same boundaries a century earlier. Lions attacked the camels that carried grain for Xerxes' army on its march into Greece, he says, and there are many lions in that region. Their limit is the Nestus, which flows through Abdera, and the Achelous, which flows through Acarnania. No lion is seen east of the Nestus or west of the Achelous (7.125–126).

## The point of the note

The scholion reads the simile as accurate natural history: Homer gives Artemis the game that the Peloponnesian mountains actually hold, and Aristotle confirms it. Homer does not explain why the hunting goddess hunts boars and deer here. Lions are common elsewhere in his similes. One follows at once in Book 6: Odysseus comes out of the thicket “like a mountain-bred lion” going after cattle, sheep or wild deer (6.130–134; see [[the-lion-and-the-girls]]). The contrast between the two similes is our observation. The scholion does not make it.
