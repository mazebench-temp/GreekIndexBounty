A scholion (σχόλιον, “note”) explains a word, a form, a reading or a problem of the text. The scholia are anonymous compilations. Many notes are abbreviated from older works, and the same note often survives in several manuscripts in different forms. Modern editions name the manuscripts that carry each note.

For the Odyssey this project cites the edition of Wilhelm Dindorf, *Scholia Graeca in Homeri Odysseam* (Oxford 1855), by volume and page. Dindorf marks each note with the letters of the manuscripts in which it appears, for example H, P, Q, E, T, V and B. A note introduced by ὅτι, “because”, usually explains a critical sign of Aristarchus.
