{{quote:Od. 6.242-243 | Before and now}}

Nausicaa contrasts the man she first saw with the man who now sits on the shore: “for before, he truly seemed to me to be unsightly, / but now he is like the gods who hold the wide sky” (6.242–243). The verb of the first line is δέατʼ, the elided form of δέατο, “he seemed”. In the Iliad and the Odyssey it occurs only here. ἀεικέλιος means “unseemly, wretched to look at”. It describes the naked man, crusted with brine, who came out of the thicket (6.127–137; see [[the-lion-and-the-girls]]).

## The reading

- **δέατʼ.** The base text (Murray) and the Oxford text of Monro and Allen print δέατʼ. The scholia explain it and record no other reading. Ms. E glosses ἐδέατο as ἐδόκει, “seemed”. Ms. H gives ἔδοξε, and mss. T and V give ἐδόκει, ἐφαίνετο, “seemed, appeared” (Dindorf p. 314). Eustathius paraphrases the line as ἀεικέλιος ἐδόκει εἶναι (p. 251).
- **δόατʼ.** The apparatus of Monro and Allen notes “242 δέατʼ L⁸”, with a small ο printed above the ε. One manuscript, L⁸, thus carries δόατʼ as a superscript correction or variant. No other witness for δόατʼ was found in the sources consulted, and no ancient critic is named for it.

The superscript ο looks like a scribe's adjustment toward a familiar form. Homer often has δοάσσατο, “it seemed”, in the formula of decision “so, as he pondered, it seemed better to him”. Book 6 has it at 6.145, when Odysseus decides to plead from a distance (see [[decision-scene]]). This explanation of the variant is our inference. The sources do not state it.

## The word

LSJ gives δέατο the meaning “seemed”. Apart from the lexicon of Hesychius, it cites no literary text but this line. The verb survives in inscriptions from Arcadia, at Tegea and at Orchomenus, with forms such as δέατοι and δεάσητοι, and the lexicographer Hesychius glosses δέαται as δοκεῖ, “seems” (LSJ s.v. δέατο). LSJ refers it to a root *deya-* “appear”, and compares δῆλος, “visible”, and δοάσσατο. Merry and Riddell take δέατο as perhaps an imperfect from a stem *dea-*, with a by-form *doa-*. Following Curtius, they refer both stems to an extension of the root “shine” (note on 6.242, p. 273). These connections are modern proposals. The ancient grammarian in ms. E derives the form from δέω/δεύω instead (Dindorf p. 314).

If the etymology is right, δέατο and δοάσσατο are two forms of one verb of appearing, and the scribe of L⁸ replaced the rare form with the one he knew. The reading of the text stays δέατʼ.
