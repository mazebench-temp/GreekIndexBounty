## Meaning

ἐπίστιον occurs once in Homer, in Nausicaa's description of the harbor of the Phaeacians: the ships are drawn up along the road, “for all have a ship-shed, each man his own” (πᾶσιν γὰρ ἐπίστιόν ἐστιν ἑκάστῳ, 6.265).

The sources agree on the thing. The word names the place on shore where a man's ship lies when it is drawn up:

- LSJ: “slip or shed for a ship”.
- The scholia: ἐποίκιον, σκηνή, νεώριον, “a shelter, a covered shed, a ship-house” (schol. E.T.V. on 6.265, Dindorf p. 316).
- Eustathius: “something like ἐποίκιον”, also called νεώριον and “houses of ships” (on 6.265, Stallbaum vol. 1, p. 253). He cites Aelius Dionysius for the Ionic word νεῶνες and the Attic νεώσοικοι, “ship-houses”.
- Merry and Riddell translate “slip”. They picture either a line of covered sheds or, “more likely”, a line of open slips on which the ships were hauled up (note on 6.265, p. 276).

The project's lexicon and translation give “ship-shed”, with “a slip or shed” (“berth”) in the lexicon note. “Slip”, “shed” and “berth” all fit the evidence. The text does not say whether the berth had a roof.

The derivation is disputed. There are three ancient and modern views:

1. **From ἑστία, “hearth”.** ἐπίστιον is the Ionic, unaspirated form of ἐφέστιον, “a place at one's own hearth”. Aristarchus explained it so in a scholion on *Iliad* 2.125, as reported by LSJ. Eustathius gives the same account and compares ἐπίστιος in Herodotus for ἐφέστιος. One scholion adds a sense from this derivation: no one among the Phaeacians is a stranger, and every man has a hearth (schol. Q on 6.265). Against this view LSJ and Merry and Riddell note that Homer elsewhere always uses the form ἐφέστιος (3.234; 7.248; 23.55; *Iliad* 2.125).
2. **From ἱστίον, “sail”.** The scholia give this derivation (παρὰ τὸ ἱστίον), and Merry and Riddell report a reading ἐπʼ ἱστίον in the scholion on *Iliad* 2.125.
3. **From ἐπί + στῆναι, “halt”.** Merry and Riddell suggest, as “perhaps the simplest solution”, a “halting-place”, from ἐπίστασις, and compare περίστια (note on 6.265, p. 276).
