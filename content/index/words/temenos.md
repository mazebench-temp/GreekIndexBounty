## Meaning

τέμενος is formed from τέμνω, “cut”. LSJ gives two senses, and both are in Homer:

1. **“A piece of land cut off and assigned as an official domain, esp. to kings and chiefs.”** The land is given by the people as a reward, and it is planted with vines and fruit trees and sown with grain.
2. **“A piece of land marked off from common uses and dedicated to a god, precinct.”** In Homer the god's τέμενος goes with an altar.

**The domain of a man.** The Iliad shows how a τέμενος is given and held:

- The Lycians “cut a τέμενος for him, outstanding among others, fine in orchard and plowland”, when they gave Bellerophon a share of the kingdom (*Iliad* 6.193–195).
- The elders of the Aetolians offered Meleager “a beautiful τέμενος of fifty acres”, half vineyard and half plowland, if he would return to battle (*Iliad* 9.574–580).
- Sarpedon reminds Glaucus that the Lycians honor them with seats, meat and full cups, and that “we hold a great τέμενος beside the banks of the Xanthus” (*Iliad* 12.310–314). The honor obliges them to fight in the front rank.
- Achilles taunts Aeneas: have the Trojans “cut a τέμενος for you, outstanding among others”, if you kill me (*Iliad* 20.184–186)?
- On the shield of Achilles is “a king's τέμενος” (τέμενος βασιλήϊον), where laborers reap and the king stands among them, glad at heart (*Iliad* 18.550–560).

In the Odyssey the τέμενος belongs to the house of the king. Alcinous has one near the city (6.293; see the note on Book 6). In the underworld Anticleia tells Odysseus that Telemachus “holds the domains in peace” (τεμένεα νέμεται) and shares in the feasts, while no one has yet taken Odysseus' “fine honor” (γέρας, 11.184–187). On Ithaca the dung heap before the gate, where the old dog Argos lies, waits for Odysseus' servants to carry it off to manure “the great τέμενος” (17.296–299).

**The precinct of a god.** The formula τέμενος βωμός τε θυήεις, “a precinct and a fragrant altar”, describes the places of Zeus on Gargarus (*Iliad* 8.48), of the river Spercheius at its springs (*Iliad* 23.148), and of Aphrodite at Paphos (8.363). The Catalogue names “the precinct of Demeter” at Pyrasus (*Iliad* 2.696).

## Outside Homer

Thucydides records a τέμενος of Alcinous on Corcyra in his own time. In 427 BC Peithias, a leader of the people, accused the five richest men of the city of “cutting vine-props from the precinct of Zeus and that of Alcinous”, for which the fine was a stater for each prop (Thucydides 3.70.4). Here τέμενος stands beside the precinct of Zeus, in the second sense of the word: a sacred precinct. The Corcyraeans claimed the Phaeacians as the earlier settlers of their island (Thucydides 1.25.4; see [[alcinous]] and [[scheria]]).

## Related words

- [[geras|γέρας]] (“prize of honor”): the τέμενος is a share of land that goes with a king's honor (11.184–185; *Iliad* 12.310–314).
- [[basileus|βασιλεύς]] (“king”): the τέμενος βασιλήϊον, “a king's domain” (*Iliad* 18.550).
