## Meaning

πομπή is the noun of πέμπω (“send”). LSJ gives “conduct, escort” and “sending away, sending home”, and cites 6.290 for the second sense. After Homer the word also means a solemn procession, the sense of English “pomp”. Homer does not use it so. The person who escorts is a πομπός, and in the Odyssey also a πομπεύς (3.325, 4.362, 13.71).

In the Iliad the escort is a god's. Bellerophon goes to Lycia “under the blameless escort of the gods” (θεῶν ὑπʼ ἀμύμονι πομπῇ, *Iliad* 6.171). Deiphobus kills Hypsenor and boasts that the dead Asius will be glad on his way to Hades, “since I have given him an escort” (*Iliad* 13.414–416). Sleep and Death carry the body of Sarpedon home to Lycia as “swift escorts” (πομποῖσιν, *Iliad* 16.671, 16.681). Hermes is the πομπός of Priam on his way to Achilles (*Iliad* 24.153, 24.182, 24.437–439, 24.461).

In the Odyssey πομπή is a theme of the plot. Odysseus can reach home only if someone sends him there:

- **The plan of Zeus.** Zeus decrees that Odysseus will come home “by the escort neither of gods nor of mortal men”. He will reach Scheria on a raft, and the Phaeacians “will send him in a ship” to his own land (5.31–37).
- **Calypso and Aeolus.** Odysseus suspects that Calypso plans “something else, not an escort” (5.173), and then she plans one, a raft and a fair wind (5.233). Aeolus gives an escort (10.18), and when the companions open the bag of winds, “the escort no longer appeared” (10.79).
- **The Phaeacians.** Odysseus asks Arete and Alcinous for πομπή (7.151). Alcinous promises it (7.191–196, 7.317) and urges the Phaeacians to it (8.30–33). The escort and gifts are given “in friendship” (8.545). Their ships need no helmsmen and know the minds of men (8.555–563; see [[phaeacians]]).
- **The cost.** Nausithous foretold that Poseidon, angry because the Phaeacians are “harmless escorts of all men” (πομποὶ ἀπήμονές … ἁπάντων), would strike one of their ships “coming back from an escort” and cover their city with a mountain (8.564–569). After the ship that carried Odysseus is turned to stone, Alcinous repeats the prophecy and tells the Phaeacians to “stop escorting mortals” (13.172–183).

The escort belongs to the duties of a host (see [[xenia]]). A host feeds, bathes and clothes a guest, gives him gifts, and at last sends him on his way. Nestor offers his sons to Telemachus as πομπῆες (3.325). Menelaus states the rule with the verb: a host must “love the guest who is present, and send him on when he wishes” (ἐθέλοντα δὲ πέμπειν, 15.74).

## Related words

- [[nostos|νόστος]] (“homecoming”): the πομπή is the means and the νόστος the end. Nausicaa joins them: πομπῆς καὶ νόστοιο (6.290).
- [[xenia|ξενία]] (“hospitality”): the escort is the last service of the host.
- [[ships|νῆες]] (“ships”): the Phaeacian escort is made by ship.
