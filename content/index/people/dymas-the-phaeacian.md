## Name

Δύμας has no clear meaning in the sources consulted here. The Iliad gives the same name to a different man, the Phrygian father of Hecuba and of Asius (Iliad 16.715–720; see [[hecuba]]).

## Descriptions

- ναυσικλειτός, “famous for ships” (6.22; see [[famous-for-ships]]). LSJ glosses the word “famed for ships, famous by sea”, and cites it here and in the *Homeric Hymn to Apollo* (31, 219), where it describes Euboea (LSJ s.v. ναυσικλειτός). The epithet fits a people whose names come from seafaring (see [[phaeacians]]).

The ancient readers divided over the word. A scholion records three readings: ναυσικλειτοῖο, one word, the epithet of Dymas; Ναυσικλειτοῖ, a dative like Καλυψοῖ, which would make it the name of the girl (“the girl Nausicleito, daughter of Dymas”); and ναυσὶ κλειτοῖο, two words. The scholion prefers the first, “famous in the work of ships” (schol. P.Q. on 6.22, Dindorf p. 295; see [[daughter-of-dymas]]).

## In the Odyssey

Dymas appears only in this verse. Athena comes to Nausicaa “in the likeness of the daughter of Dymas, famous for ships, / who was of the same age as she, and was dear to her heart” (6.22–23; see [[dream-scene]]). Nothing else is told of him.
