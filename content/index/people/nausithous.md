## Etymology

Ναυσίθοος is a compound of ναῦς (“ship”), in its combining form ναυσι-, and θοός (“swift”): “swift with ships”. It belongs with the many Phaeacian names drawn from seafaring (Merry and Riddell on 8.111, p. 319; see [[phaeacians]]), and it shares its first element with the name of his granddaughter [[nausicaa|Nausicaa]].

## Family

His father is Poseidon, and his mother Periboea, “best of women in looks”, the youngest daughter of Eurymedon, who once ruled the overbearing Giants and destroyed his reckless people and himself (7.56–62). He “ruled among the Phaeacians” and had two sons, Rhexenor and [[alcinous|Alcinous]] (7.62–63). Rhexenor died young, and his only daughter [[queen-arete|Arete]] married Alcinous (7.63–66). Nausithous is therefore the grandfather of both the queen and her daughter Nausicaa. A scholion notes that Athena's account “takes up the succession of the family from far back” (schol. on 7.56, Dindorf p. 325).

## Descriptions

- θεοειδής, “godlike”, the narrator's word for him in Book 6 (6.7; see [[godlike]]).
- μεγάθυμος, “great-spirited” (7.62).
- ὁ γέρων, “the old man”, in his son's memory of his prophecy (8.570, 13.178).

## Elsewhere in the Odyssey

**The prophecy.** Twice Alcinous recalls what his father used to say: Poseidon resented the Phaeacians because they gave safe escort to all men, and one day he would shatter a Phaeacian ship on its way back from an escort and “cover our city with a great mountain” (8.564–571 ≈ 13.172–178). In Book 8 Alcinous leaves the matter to the god: “he may fulfil it, or it may stay unfulfilled, as he pleases” (8.570–571). In Book 13, when Poseidon has turned the returning ship to stone, he says that “now all these things are being fulfilled” (13.178), and he orders a sacrifice to turn away the rest (see [[the-wrath-of-poseidon]] and [[pompe]]).

## Outside Homer

**A homonym.** Hesiod gives the same name to a son of Odysseus: Calypso bore Nausithous and Nausinous to Odysseus (*Theogony* 1017–1018; see [[hesiod]]). He is a different figure from the Phaeacian founder.

**Apollonius.** In the *Argonautica* Nausithous is the king of the Phaeacians in the time of Heracles. Heracles came to his house, and to Macris, to be purified after killing his children. Heracles' son Hyllus, born on the island to the nymph Melite, did not wish to live there under the rule of Nausithous when he grew up. He gathered a band of Phaeacians and sailed to the Cronian sea, and “the lord, the hero Nausithous” prepared his journey (4.537–551; see [[apollonius-drepane]] and [[apollonius]]).
