## Who she is

Homer describes her in two verses: “the daughter of Dymas, famous for ships, / who was of the same age as she, and was dear to her heart” (6.22–23). ὁμηλικίη is properly “age-group”, here used of one person, “an age-mate” (Merry and Riddell on 6.23; a scholion glosses it ὁμῆλιξ, schol. H on 6.23, Dindorf p. 295). κεχάριστο θυμῷ, “was dear to her heart”, is the pluperfect of χαρίζομαι. She is not named, and she does not appear in person.

**A possible name.** A scholion reports that some readers took the word before Δύμαντος as her name in the dative, Ναυσικλειτοῖ, “to Nausicleito”, on the model of Καλυψοῖ. The scholion itself prefers the epithet ναυσικλειτοῖο, “famous for ships”, of her father (schol. P.Q. on 6.22, Dindorf p. 295; see [[dymas-the-phaeacian]] and [[famous-for-ships]]). Murray's text prints the epithet.
