{{quote:Od. 6.180-185 | Odysseus' blessing}}

Odysseus ends his supplication of Nausicaa with a prayer for her, since he has nothing else to give: may the gods grant her what she longs for, a husband, a home, and good ὁμοφροσύνη, “like-mindedness” (6.180–182; see [[homophrosyne]] and [[supplication]]). The maxim gives the reason. The comparative pair κρεῖσσον καὶ ἄρειον, “stronger and better”, puts the like-minded couple above every other good. The verb is in the dual, ὁμοφρονέοντε … ἔχητον: the two are one pair, and they “keep a house” together (6.183). The pair is then named, ἀνὴρ ἠδὲ γυνή, “a man and a woman”, at the start of the next verse (6.184).

**Its effects.** The maxim measures the good by its effects on others: “many sorrows to their enemies, / and joys to their well-wishers” (6.184–185). Merry and Riddell take ἄλγεα and χάρματα as accusatives in apposition to the whole clause, and they compare Livy 3.72 for the thought (note on 6.184, p. 268).

**The disputed last words.** μάλιστα δέ τʼ ἔκλυον αὐτοί is literally “and they themselves hear it most”. ἔκλυον is a gnomic aorist, a past tense used of what always happens. Two main readings are offered:

1. **“They themselves feel it most.”** A scholion explains that the couple themselves perceive and enjoy the benefit of their bond with each other (as reported by Merry and Riddell). Merry and Riddell accept this sense, with hesitation. They compare Iliad 13.734, where many men profit from the wise man, “but he himself knows it best” (μάλιστα δὲ καὐτὸς ἀνέγνω). They suggest that the couple hear the words of friends and enemies, and hear their own joy “even better by their own hearts” (note on 6.185, pp. 268–269).
2. **“They themselves have the best report.”** On this reading κλύω means “hear oneself spoken of”, so the couple are well spoken of. Merry and Riddell report Lobeck's version of this kind, that the couple call each other happy and hear themselves called happy by others. They reject it, because κλύειν alone, without εὖ, “well”, can hardly mean “be well spoken of” (note on 6.185, p. 269).

Merry and Riddell also report Nauck's judgment that the words are corrupt (verba vitiosa), and they think it “not improbable”. The translation in this library gives both readings in its notes and renders “they themselves know it best” (TRANSLATION.md, on 6.185).

**In the Odyssey.** The maxim describes the marriage that Odysseus is trying to reach. He has just refused Calypso's offer of immortality in order to go home to his wife (5.203–224). The words of the root are rare. The noun occurs once more in the plural, of the friendship between Telemachus and Pisistratus (ὁμοφροσύνῃσιν, 15.198), and the verb once more, when the blinded Cyclops wishes that his ram could think like him and speak (ὁμοφρονέοις, 9.456; see [[homophrosyne]] and [[husbands-and-wives]]). Nausicaa answers the prayer, in her way, with her own wish for “such a man” as husband (6.244–245; see [[marriage]]).
