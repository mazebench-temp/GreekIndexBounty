## Who they are

Nausicaa counts them for her father: “you have five dear sons born in the halls, / two married, and three unmarried young men in their bloom” (6.62–63). ὀπυίοντες, “married”, is the participle of ὀπυίω, used of the man who takes a wife; ἠίθεοι are unmarried young men (LSJ s.vv. ὀπυίω, ἠίθεος). θαλέθοντες, “in their bloom”, is from the root of θάλλω, “flourish”.

Book 8 names three of them, the ones who stand up for the games: “three sons of blameless Alcinous, / Laodamas and Halius and godlike Clytoneus” (8.118–119). The other two are not named. The sources consulted here do not discuss the meaning of their names.

## Descriptions

- **Laodamas.** Alcinous loves him most, and he sits next to his father; Alcinous gives his seat to Odysseus (7.170–171). He wins the boxing, proposes that the stranger be invited to compete, and is ἀγαθὸς πάϊς Ἀλκινόοιο, “the good son of Alcinous” (8.130–145). Odysseus will compete with any Phaeacian except Laodamas, because “he is my host: who would fight with a friend?” (8.204–211).
- **Halius and Laodamas.** Alcinous asks the two of them to dance alone, because no one could match them. They play with a purple ball, one throwing it up to the clouds and the other leaping to catch it, then dance while the young men beat time (8.370–380; see [[ball-play]] and [[dance]]).
- **Clytoneus.** He wins the footrace (8.120–125).
