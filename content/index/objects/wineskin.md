ἀσκός is a skin made into a bag, “especially a wineskin” (LSJ). Homer's formula is ἀσκῷ ἐν αἰγείῳ, “in a goatskin”. The Trojan heralds carry the wine for the oath of Book 3 through the city in a goatskin, with two lambs (Iliad 3.245–247). See [[wine]] and [[goats]].

The Odyssey gives the skin of wine to travelers. Calypso puts on Odysseus' raft one skin of dark wine and another, a large one, of water, with food in a bag (5.265–267). Odysseus takes a goatskin of the dark sweet wine that Maron had given him when he goes up to the cave of the Cyclops (9.196–197), and fills a great skin from it (9.212). It is the wine with which he makes the Cyclops drunk. The largest skin in the poem is the bag that Aeolus makes from the hide of a nine-year-old ox, in which he binds the winds (10.19–20). Odysseus' men open it within sight of Ithaca, and the winds drive the ship back (10.46–55).

In later Greek ἀσκός is also any leather bag, a paunch, a pair of bellows or a bagpipe (LSJ).
